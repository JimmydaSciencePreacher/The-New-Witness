## 4. Romans 9–11 — promise, mercy, calling, proclamation, and the nations

Romans 9–11 is one connected argument. Romans 9 cannot be treated as a detached philosophical statement about individual choice; neither can it be reduced to a discussion of historical usefulness while ignoring Paul's language of promise, mercy, calling, wrath, glory, and salvation.

### 4.1 Romans 9 — God's word has not failed

Paul begins with grief for his kinsmen according to the flesh and lists Israel's privileges (Romans 9:1–5). The question is whether God's word and promises have failed because many Israelites do not believe.

Paul answers:

> For they are not all Israel, which are of Israel. (Romans 9:6, KJV)

He distinguishes physical descent from the children of promise. Isaac and the promise to Sarah are followed by Rebecca's twins. Before the children had done good or evil, God's purpose according to election was declared to stand “not of works, but of him that calleth” (9:11).

Paul then speaks of God's mercy (9:14–16), Pharaoh and the declaration of God's power (9:17–18), the creature/Creator distinction and potter/clay (9:19–21), and the vessels of wrath and mercy (9:22–23). Verse 24 identifies the called as including Jews and Gentiles.

**Direct finding:** Paul explicitly locates the purpose according to election in God who calls, and mercy in God who shows mercy. Verse 16 does not make human willing or effort the source of mercy.

**Necessary boundary:** Genesis 25:23 concerns two nations in Rebecca's womb, and Malachi 1:2–5 addresses Jacob/Israel and Esau/Edom in their historical setting. Paul nevertheless names the twins and emphasizes that the purpose was declared before they had done good or evil. Both the corporate/national and the named-person dimensions must remain visible. Romans 9:6–24 does not, by itself, exhaustively define the complete relation between corporate and individual election.

**Grammatical safeguard:** Romans 9:22 describes vessels of wrath as “fitted to destruction” with a middle/passive participle and no expressed agent in that clause. Verse 23 explicitly says God “afore prepared” the vessels of mercy, using an active verb with God as subject. The clauses must not be made grammatically identical. The absence of an expressed agent in verse 22 does not prove an alternative agent; it means the agent is not stated in that clause.

### 4.2 Romans 10 — the Gospel is proclaimed, heard, believed, and confessed

Romans 10 must not be erased by Romans 9.

Paul speaks of Israel's zeal, Christ, the word of faith, believing, confessing, calling upon the Lord, hearing, and preaching (10:1–17). He asks how people can believe in the One of whom they have not heard, and how they can hear without a preacher. He then states:

> So then faith cometh by hearing, and hearing by the word of God. (Romans 10:17, KJV)

**Direct finding:** The Gospel's proclamation and the hearer's response are real and integral to Paul's account. The preacher is a genuine instrument of proclamation, not the originating Author of salvation. Romans 10 does not say autonomous human will originates God's mercy or purpose.

### 4.3 Romans 11 — remnant, partial hardening, Gentile inclusion, and mercy

Romans 11 continues Paul's answer to whether God has cast away His people. Paul points to the remnant according to the election of grace (11:1–6). He describes the hardening of the rest (11:7–10), but insists that Israel's stumbling is not presented as an absolute final rejection (11:11–15).

The olive-tree image warns Gentile believers not to boast over the branches (11:16–24). Paul then identifies a mystery: blindness in part has happened to Israel until the fulness of the Gentiles comes in (11:25). He speaks of God's covenant, mercy, and the disobedience of both Jews and Gentiles (11:26–32), then ends in praise of the depth of God's wisdom and knowledge (11:33–36).

**Direct finding:** Romans 11 preserves the remnant, partial—not total—hardening, Gentile inclusion, God's continuing purpose toward Israel, and the dependence of all upon divine mercy. Gentile believers are specifically warned against boasting.

**Boundary:** Romans 11:32 must be read within Paul's argument about Jews, Gentiles, disobedience, and mercy. The word “all” in that context cannot simply be used as a standalone proof of universal final salvation for every individual.

### 4.4 Romans 9–11 together

| Chapter | Emphasis | What must be preserved |
|---|---|---|
| Romans 9 | Promise, election, mercy, hardening, calling | God's purpose and mercy; Israel's privileges; the Creator/creature distinction; grammatical limits |
| Romans 10 | Christ, Gospel, believing, confessing, hearing, preaching | Real human response and the public proclamation of the Word |
| Romans 11 | Remnant, partial hardening, Gentile inclusion, mercy | God's continuing purpose, the warning against Gentile boasting, and the final doxology |

**Combined finding:** God's purpose and mercy are not sourced in independent human willing. Yet the same apostolic argument explicitly includes the preached Word, hearing, believing, confessing, and calling upon the Lord. The chapters must be read as one argument rather than used against one another.

## 5. John 6:35–45, 63–65 — the Father gives, draws, teaches, and grants

John 6 follows the feeding of the five thousand and Jesus' walking on the sea. The crowd seeks Him, and Jesus challenges them concerning why they seek Him (6:22–27). He identifies Himself as the bread of life (6:35).

The discourse includes both the human response and the Father's action:

- The one who comes to Jesus shall not hunger; the one who believes shall not thirst (6:35).
- Some have seen Jesus and yet do not believe (6:36).
- All that the Father gives to the Son shall come to Him; the Son will not cast out the one who comes (6:37).
- The Son came to do the will of the Father who sent Him (6:38).
- The Father's will is that the Son lose nothing of all that the Father has given Him, but raise it at the last day (6:39).
- Everyone who sees the Son and believes has everlasting life, and the Son will raise him at the last day (6:40).
- No one can come unless the Father draws him (6:44).
- The one who has heard and learned from the Father comes to the Son (6:45).
- The Spirit quickens; the flesh profits nothing; Jesus' words are spirit and life (6:63).
- Some hearers do not believe (6:64).
- No one can come unless it is given to him by the Father (6:65).

### 5.1 The discourse explains its own language

Verse 45 is essential. Jesus does not leave the word “draw” in verse 44 without explanation: He connects the Father's drawing with being taught of God, hearing, learning, and coming to Christ.

Verse 65 restates the necessity of the Father's action in terms of what is given. Verse 36 and verse 64 prevent the assumption that seeing Jesus, hearing outwardly, or being physically near Him automatically produces believing.

### 5.2 The Father's giving and the Son's receiving

The repeated statements in verses 37–40 are not incidental:

- The Father gives.
- Those given come.
- The Son receives the one who comes and does not cast him out.
- The Son loses nothing of what the Father has given Him.
- The Son raises them at the last day.

The Son's mission is expressly aligned with the Father's will. The text does not portray the Father as merely responding after people independently arrive at Christ.

### 5.3 Original-language observations and limits

- **δίδωμι (*didōmi*)** — “give.” The Father is the giver in 6:37, 39 and 65; the Son is the recipient.
- **ἥξει (*hēxei*)** — “shall come” (6:37). The coming of those given is stated as certain in the sentence.
- **ἑλκύω (*helkyō*)** — “draw” (6:44). The lexical range and the relation to John 12:32 must be considered; the verb alone should not be made to settle every question about the scope or manner of drawing.
- **ἀκούω (*akouō*)** and **μανθάνω (*manthanō*)** — “hear” and “learn” (6:45). Jesus connects the Father's teaching with coming to Him.
- **πιστεύω (*pisteuō*)** — “believe” (6:35, 40, 64). Believing is a real response, not a word to be removed from the account.
- **ζῳοποιέω (*zōopoieō*)** — “make alive / give life” (6:63). Jesus identifies the Spirit as the One who gives life.

The Greek perfect passive form in 6:65 presents coming as something given; it does not, by tense alone, supply a complete chronology of the giving, drawing, quickening, and first act of faith.

John 12:32 uses the same drawing verb in a different context. It must be considered when discussing the lexical scope of “draw”; it does not erase the explicit necessity of the Father's drawing and grant in John 6.

**Finding:** John 6 explicitly joins the Father's giving, drawing, teaching, and granting with the Son's receiving and preserving, the Spirit's life-giving work, and the real responses of coming and believing. It rejects autonomous human initiative as the originating cause of coming to Christ. It does not provide an exhaustive technical timeline of every aspect of salvation.

## 6. John 10:22–30 — the sheep hear, are known, follow, and are kept

The immediate setting is the Feast of Dedication in Jerusalem. The hearers ask Jesus to tell them plainly whether He is the Christ (10:22–24). Jesus points to His works in the Father's name (10:25), then says:

> But ye believe not, because ye are not of my sheep, as I said unto you. (John 10:26, KJV)

He describes His sheep:

> My sheep hear my voice, and I know them, and they follow me: (John 10:27, KJV)

He continues that He gives them eternal life, they shall never perish, and no one will pluck them from His hand (10:28). The Father, who gave them to the Son, is greater than all, and no one is able to pluck them from the Father's hand (10:29). Jesus concludes, “I and my Father are one” (10:30).

### 6.1 The causal statement in verse 26

The “because” must not be reversed. Jesus does not say in this sentence that they are not His sheep because they failed to believe. He says they do not believe because they are not of His sheep.

This is a strong statement about the relation between sheep identity and unbelief in this discourse. It must be retained without making it answer every question about the full chronology of election, calling, regeneration, and faith.

### 6.2 The sheep's response is real

The sheep hear. Jesus knows them. They follow. These are active statements about the sheep. Divine initiative does not make their hearing and following unreal or unnecessary.

### 6.3 The keeping is emphatic

Jesus gives eternal life and says His sheep shall never perish. No one can snatch them from His hand; no one can snatch them from the Father's hand.

The assurance is not merely that preservation might happen if the sheep can independently secure it. Jesus identifies Himself as the giver of eternal life and the Father as the One who gave the sheep to Him.

### 6.4 Original-language observations

- **ὅτι (*hoti*)** — “because” (10:26); introduces Jesus' stated reason for their unbelief.
- **ἀκούουσιν (*akouousin*)** — “they hear” (10:27), present active indicative.
- **γινώσκω (*ginōskō*)** — “I know” (10:27), Jesus' own knowledge of His sheep.
- **ἀκολουθοῦσιν (*akolouthousin*)** — “they follow” (10:27), present active indicative; the sheep's following is real.
- **δίδωμι (*didōmi*)** — “I give” (10:28); Jesus is the giver of eternal life.
- **οὐ μὴ ἀπόλωνται (*ou mē apolōntai*)** — “they shall never perish” (10:28), emphatic negation.
- **δέδωκέν (*dedōken*)** — “gave” (10:29), perfect active indicative; the Father is explicitly the giver and the Son the recipient. The form presents the giving as an accomplished act with continuing relevance, but does not identify its calendar date.
- **ἁρπάζω (*harpazō*)** — “snatch/pluck”; the passage denies that anyone can seize the sheep from the Son's or Father's hand.

“I and my Father are one” must not be used to collapse the Father and Son into one person. The sentence retains the plural “we” while affirming their unity.

**Finding:** John 10 joins belonging to Christ with hearing, being known, and following. The Father gives the sheep to the Son; the Son gives them eternal life and keeps them; no one can snatch them from either divine hand.

## 7. John 17:1–26 — the given, the kept, and those who believe through the word

John 17 is the Son's prayer to the Father as the appointed hour approaches. It must be read as a prayer in its own setting, not merely as a list of isolated statements about giving.

### 7.1 Authority and eternal life — verses 1–5

Jesus asks the Father to glorify the Son, that the Son may glorify the Father. The Father has given the Son power over all flesh, that He should give eternal life to as many as the Father has given Him (17:2).

Jesus identifies eternal life in terms of knowing the only true God and Jesus Christ whom He has sent (17:3). He then says:

> I have glorified thee on the earth: I have finished the work which thou gavest me to do. (John 17:4, KJV)

The Father gives the work; the Son accomplishes it. Verse 5 speaks of the glory the Son had with the Father before the world was.

**Finding:** The Father's giving of authority and work, and the Son's accomplishment and giving of eternal life, are explicit. The mission is not originated by the disciples or by those who later believe.

### 7.2 The men given out of the world — verses 6–10

Jesus says He manifested the Father's name to the men given Him out of the world. They were the Father's, and the Father gave them to the Son (17:6). They have kept the Father's word; they have known that what the Father gave the Son came from the Father (17:7–8).

Jesus says He prays for those given Him, not for the world in the same immediate sense (17:9), and states that all the Son's are the Father's and all the Father's are the Son's (17:10).

The passage identifies a particular group of disciples in the immediate prayer context. It should not be flattened into an assumption that every use of “given” in John refers to precisely the same group or function without examining the context.

### 7.3 Keeping — verses 11–19

Jesus asks the Holy Father to keep those given Him through the Father's name (17:11). In verse 12 He says that while He was with them He kept them in the Father's name and guarded them; none is lost except the son of perdition, that Scripture might be fulfilled.

The exception must remain in the record. John 6:70–71 identifies Judas among the Twelve and speaks of the betrayer; John 13:18 also places the betrayal in relation to Scripture. The text does not permit us to erase John 17:12's exception or to assume that every category—apostolic selection, discipleship, sheep, and the saving people kept by Christ—is automatically identical.

Jesus asks that His own be kept from the evil (17:15), sanctified through the truth, and identifies the Father's word as truth (17:17). He sends them into the world as the Father sent Him (17:18), and speaks of sanctifying Himself for their sakes (17:19).

**Finding:** The Son's keeping and His request for the Father's keeping are explicit. The keeping is connected to the Father's name and truth, and to the Son's mission in the world.

### 7.4 Those who will believe through the disciples' word — verses 20–23

Jesus explicitly extends His prayer beyond the immediate disciples:

> Neither pray I for these alone, but for them also which shall believe on me through their word; (John 17:20, KJV)

This verse is essential. The prayer includes future believers whose believing comes through the disciples' word. Their faith is real; the word through which they believe is also identified.

Jesus prays for their unity, that the world may believe that the Father sent the Son (17:21), and speaks of the glory given to Him and shared with them (17:22–23).

**Finding:** John 17 does not restrict the prayer's horizon to the original apostolic group. Jesus explicitly includes those who will believe through their word.

### 7.5 The given and the Son's glory — verses 24–26

Jesus asks that those whom the Father has given Him be with Him where He is, to behold His glory (17:24). He distinguishes the world that has not known the Father from those who have known that the Father sent the Son (17:25). He has declared the Father's name to them and will continue to declare it, that the Father's love may be in them and the Son in them (17:26).

**Finding:** The prayer's end is communion with the Son, beholding His glory, and the Father's love in His people.

### 7.6 Original-language observations and boundaries

- **δίδωμι (*didōmi*)** — “give”; repeated throughout the prayer for authority, work, people, words, and glory. The object and context must be identified each time.
- **τηρέω (*tēreō*)** — “keep/guard/observe”; used in the prayer's language of keeping. The precise object and requested action must be read in each sentence.
- **κόσμος (*kosmos*)** — “world”; its use in John 17 varies by context and should not be forced into one simple referent in every verse.
- **πιστεύω (*pisteuō*)** — “believe”; 17:20 expressly includes future believers through the disciples' word.
- **ἵνα (*hina*)** — “that/so that”; introduces purpose clauses throughout the prayer. The stated purposes should not be replaced with an invented chronology.

John 17:12's son of perdition exception, John 17:20's future believers, and John 17:24's given people must all be retained. The prayer does not supply a precise timestamp for when each person was given to the Son.

### Combined finding

The canonical witness is coherent:

1. The Father purposes and gives.
2. The Son is sent and receives those given.
3. The Father draws, teaches, and grants; the Spirit gives life.
4. Those who belong to the Son come, hear, believe, and follow.
5. The Son gives eternal life, keeps His own, loses nothing of what the Father has given Him, and raises them at the last day.
6. The apostolic word is the means through which future believers hear and believe.
7. The Son's prayer asks the Father to keep His people and brings the prayer to its stated end: that they be with Him and behold His glory.

The passages do not say that human response originates the Father's giving. Nor do they portray hearing, believing, and following as unreal. Divine initiative and genuine human response are both in the text.

## 9. What “the Father's giving” does and does not establish

### Established

- The Father is explicitly identified as the giver of people to the Son (John 6:37, 39; 10:29; 17:6, 9, 24).
- Those given come to the Son (John 6:37).
- No one can come unless the Father draws and grants (John 6:44–45, 65).
- The sheep hear Jesus' voice and follow Him (John 10:27).
- The Son gives eternal life and emphatically promises that His sheep will never perish (John 10:28).
- The Son says He loses nothing of all the Father has given Him and will raise it at the last day (John 6:39–40).
- Jesus asks the Father to keep those given Him (John 17:9–12).
- The prayer explicitly includes future believers through the disciples' word (John 17:20).
- Romans 9–11 places election, mercy, calling, Gospel proclamation, believing, the remnant, and Gentile inclusion within Paul's connected argument.

### Not established by these passages alone

- The exact temporal date at which each individual is given to the Son.
- A complete technical chronology of giving, drawing, regeneration, first faith, justification, sealing, and perseverance.
- That “given,” “chosen,” “sheep,” “disciple,” “apostle,” and “believer” are interchangeable categories in every passage.
- That the Father's giving is itself the same event as the writing of names in the Book of Life.
- Universal final salvation for every individual from Romans 11:32 alone.
- The agent in Romans 9:22's passive participial clause.
- A single exhaustive lexical definition of “draw” that settles every context in which the word occurs.

The absence of a timestamp is a limit of the passages examined, not evidence that the Father's purpose is uncertain or that the completed GUA-W is unfinished.

## 11. Recognized relationship within GUA-W

The completed GUA-W remains the reference. The relationship recognized here belongs within the established order:

**Author → Word → Creation → Witness**

- **Author:** The Father purposes, sends, gives, draws, teaches, and keeps according to His will.
- **Word:** The Son is sent by the Father, receives those given, manifests the Father's name, accomplishes the work given Him, gives eternal life, and raises His own.
- **Creation:** The Gospel is proclaimed in the world; hearers hear, learn, come, believe, and follow. The Spirit gives life.
- **Witness:** The disciples receive the word and testify; future believers believe through their word. The Witness registers what is given and does not become its originating cause.

No new GUA-W component is introduced. This is a recognition of the Father's initiative, the Son's accomplishment and keeping, the Spirit's life-giving work, and the genuine response of those who hear and follow.

The next question arises from the boundaries already identified:

**How should the Father's giving of people to the Son be compared with the Book of Life passages—especially Revelation 13:8, 17:8, 20:12–15, and 21:27—without treating the giving, election, foreordination, and inscription of names as interchangeable statements?**

That investigation must return to the source repository's existing Book of Life examination and preserve the grammatical ambiguity in Revelation 13:8 and the clearer wording in Revelation 17:8. It must not infer an inscription date from John 6 or John 17 alone.

## 13. Conclusion

The question was: What does Scripture reveal about the Father's giving of people to the Son, and how does that giving relate to calling, believing, following, and the keeping of those who belong to Christ?

**The passages present the Father's giving as divine initiative, not as a passive response to autonomous human arrival.** John 6 says those given shall come, that no one can come unless the Father draws, and that coming is granted by the Father. John 10 says Jesus' sheep hear His voice and follow Him, that He gives them eternal life, and that no one can snatch them from His or the Father's hand. John 17 presents the Father's giving, the Son's accomplishing and keeping, and the extension of the prayer to those who will believe through the disciples' word. Romans 9–11 preserves God's purpose, mercy, and calling while also describing the real proclamation of the Gospel, hearing, believing, confessing, the remnant, partial hardening, and Gentile inclusion.

The conclusion must keep both sides of the testimony:

**The Father gives. The Son receives, keeps, gives life, and raises. The Spirit gives life. The sheep hear and follow. The Gospel is proclaimed; people hear and believe.**

The creature's response is real. It is not the Author of the Father's purpose.

The exact temporal moment of the giving, the full chronology of salvation, and the relation between the Father's giving and the Book of Life inscription remain bounded questions for their own passage-level examination.

**The source remains the reference. The Garden remains the record.**
