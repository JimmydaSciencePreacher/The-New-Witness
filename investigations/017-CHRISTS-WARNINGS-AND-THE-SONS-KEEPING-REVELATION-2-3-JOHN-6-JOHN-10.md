## 1. The question

Investigation 016 carried forward this unresolved question:

**How do Christ's warnings to the seven churches relate to John 6:37–40 and John 10:27–30 without weakening either the warnings or the Son's promises?**

The investigation must not solve the tension by deleting one side of Scripture.

- The warnings in Revelation 2–3 are real. Christ calls churches and people to repent, wake, hold fast, remain faithful, and hear. He announces consequences for refusal and compromise.
- The promises in John 6 and John 10 are also real. The Father gives people to the Son; those given come; the Son receives them, loses nothing of what the Father has given Him, gives eternal life, and says His sheep will never perish and cannot be snatched from His hand.

The task is to identify the passages' respective subjects, addressees, claims, and contexts, then establish what relationship the canon supports. We must not assume that every person associated with a visible congregation is thereby identified as one of Christ's sheep; nor may we assume that every warning is merely hypothetical or without consequence.

## 4. First observation: the seven messages address real churches in real conditions

Revelation 2–3 is not an abstract essay about an invisible category. The risen Christ addresses seven named churches and evaluates their actual condition.

The messages include:

- commendation for labour, endurance, faith, love, service, patience, and fidelity;
- rebuke for abandoned love, tolerated false teaching, spiritual deadness, and lukewarm self-sufficiency;
- commands to remember, repent, wake, strengthen, hold fast, be faithful, and hear;
- warnings of removal, conflict, judgment, suffering, sudden visitation, and rejection;
- promises to the overcomer.

The warnings are not all identical, and neither are their consequences.

### 4.1 Ephesus — a church can lose its candlestick (Revelation 2:1–7)

Christ recognizes labour, patience, discernment, and endurance, but says they have left their first love. He commands them to remember, repent, and do the first works. Otherwise He will remove their candlestick out of its place (2:5).

**Direct statement:** Christ warns of the removal of the candlestick if they do not repent.

**Boundary:** The image is addressed to the church as a church. The verse does not say that every individual associated with Ephesus is thereby lost eternally. Nor should the removal of a candlestick be casually equated with the second death. The text identifies a serious corporate consequence and leaves the exact relation to each individual's final state unstated.

### 4.2 Smyrna — suffering is not evidence of abandonment (Revelation 2:8–11)

Christ warns that some will be cast into prison and suffer tribulation. He commands faithfulness unto death and promises the crown of life. The overcomer will not be hurt by the second death (2:10–11).

**Direct statement:** The church is warned about real persecution, and Christ gives a real promise concerning the second death.

**Boundary:** The promise does not mean the faithful will avoid physical death. The message explicitly contemplates faithfulness unto death. Physical death and the second death must not be confused.

### 4.3 Pergamos — Christ warns against tolerated compromise (Revelation 2:12–17)

Christ commends those who hold fast His name and have not denied His faith, even in the days of Antipas. He also rebukes those who hold the doctrine of Balaam and the Nicolaitans. He commands repentance and warns that He will fight against them with the sword of His mouth (2:14–16).

**Direct statement:** Christ distinguishes faithful testimony from tolerated corrupt teaching within the same church.

**Boundary:** The text does not require us to say that every person in Pergamos held the condemned teaching. The message itself distinguishes those who hold fast from those who compromise.

### 4.4 Thyatira — Christ gives space to repent and announces judgment (Revelation 2:18–29)

Christ recognizes love, service, faith, patience, and increasing works. Yet He rebukes the church for tolerating the woman called Jezebel and her corrupt teaching. He says He gave her space to repent, but she did not repent (2:20–21). Judgment is announced against her and those who commit adultery with her unless they repent (2:22–23).

To the rest who do not hold the doctrine, Christ says He lays no other burden upon them, but commands them to hold fast what they have until He comes (2:24–25).

**Direct statement:** Christ distinguishes the unrepentant teacher and those following her from “the rest” who do not hold that doctrine. The warning and the command to hold fast are both explicit.

**Boundary:** Do not collapse the whole congregation into the same spiritual condition. Christ Himself distinguishes groups within the message.

### 4.5 Sardis — reputation does not overrule Christ's assessment (Revelation 3:1–6)

The church has a name that it lives, but Christ says it is dead. He commands watchfulness, strengthening, remembrance, holding fast, and repentance (3:1–3). He warns that if they do not watch, He will come as a thief. He also recognizes a few who have not defiled their garments (3:4).

**Direct statement:** A public reputation of life is not sufficient when Christ declares the actual condition otherwise. The warning is addressed to the church, while Christ recognizes a faithful few.

**Boundary:** The message does not authorize an outside observer to pronounce the final state of every person in Sardis. Christ's own knowledge and judgment remain decisive.

### 4.6 Philadelphia — Christ keeps, and commands them to hold fast (Revelation 3:7–13)

Christ recognizes that they have little strength, have kept His word, and have not denied His name (3:8). Because they have kept the word of His patience, He promises to keep them from the hour of temptation (3:10). He then says, “Behold, I come quickly: hold that fast which thou hast, that no man take thy crown” (3:11).

**Direct statement:** The same message contains Christ's promise to keep them and His command that they hold fast.

This is an especially important passage for the present question. Divine keeping and commanded perseverance are not presented as rivals. Christ's promise does not make the command meaningless; the command does not make Christ's keeping uncertain.

**Boundary:** The verse does not define “crown” as a technical synonym for eternal life, nor does it state that losing the crown is identical to being plucked from Christ's hand. Do not insert that equation.

### 4.7 Laodicea — rebuke and chastening are directed toward repentance (Revelation 3:14–22)

Laodicea considers itself rich and in need of nothing, but Christ declares it wretched, miserable, poor, blind, and naked (3:17). He counsels them to receive what they lack from Him (3:18). He then says:

> As many as I love, I rebuke and chasten: be zealous therefore, and repent. (Revelation 3:19, KJV)

He presents Himself at the door, calling for the one who hears His voice and opens to Him (3:20).

**Direct statement:** Christ identifies His rebuke and chastening with love and calls for repentance.

**Supported inference:** A warning or rebuke from Christ is not automatically equivalent to a declaration that the person addressed has been finally rejected. In this message, rebuke and chastening are expressly directed toward repentance.

**Boundary:** This does not mean every warning in Scripture is merely corrective discipline. The other messages also contain announced judgments and serious consequences.

## 5. John 6 must be read beyond verses 37–40

The assurance in John 6:37–40 is emphatic. But the same discourse continues through verses 60–71, where the response of the hearers is exposed. The complete discourse is essential.

### 5.1 The promise — John 6:35–40

Jesus says that the one who comes to Him will not hunger, and the one who believes on Him will never thirst (6:35). He also says some have seen Him and yet do not believe (6:36).

Then He states:

- All that the Father gives Him shall come to Him (6:37).
- The one who comes to Him He will in no wise cast out (6:37).
- He came down from heaven to do the will of the Father who sent Him (6:38).
- The Father's will is that He lose nothing of all that the Father has given Him, but raise it up at the last day (6:39).
- Everyone who sees the Son and believes on Him has everlasting life, and the Son will raise him at the last day (6:40).

The promise must be left at full strength. The Son does not fail to accomplish the Father's will concerning those given to Him.

### 5.2 The Father's drawing and teaching — John 6:41–59

Jesus says no man can come to Him except the Father draw him (6:44). He then connects the Father's action with being taught of God: every one who has heard and learned of the Father comes to Him (6:45). In verse 65 He repeats that no man can come unless it is given to him of the Father.

The discourse also speaks of the Spirit giving life (6:63).

**Direct statement:** Coming to Christ is related to the Father's drawing, teaching, and granting. The Spirit gives life. The human response of coming and believing remains explicit.

### 5.3 The departure of many disciples — John 6:60–66

Some of the disciples say the saying is hard and ask who can hear it (6:60). Jesus responds and speaks of the Spirit giving life (6:63). He then says that some among them do not believe (6:64), and the text states that Jesus knew from the beginning who they were that believed not and who should betray Him.

After Jesus repeats that no one can come unless it is given by the Father (6:65), the passage records:

> From that time many of his disciples went back, and walked no more with him. (John 6:66, KJV)

This must not be omitted. The chapter itself contains both the promise that the Son loses nothing of all the Father has given Him and the report that many disciples turned back.

### 5.4 What the chapter does and does not identify

The text calls the departing group “many of his disciples” (6:66). It also says that some among the hearers did not believe (6:64). It does not expressly identify those who departed in verse 66 as the same persons given by the Father in verse 37.

The chapter therefore gives us a necessary distinction:

- There are people in the wider circle of hearers and disciples who see, hear, and associate with Jesus, yet do not believe or continue with Him.
- Jesus describes those given by the Father as coming to Him, and declares that He will not cast out the one who comes and will lose nothing of all that the Father has given Him.
- The discourse does not say that the Father-given are lost when the wider group departs.

The text must not be made to say that every person ever called a disciple is necessarily one of the sheep or one of those given in the saving sense. Nor should we claim to know the final state of every person in the departing crowd beyond the statements made.

### 5.5 Peter's response and Judas — John 6:67–71

Jesus asks the Twelve whether they will also go away (6:67). Peter answers, “Lord, to whom shall we go? thou hast the words of eternal life” (6:68), and confesses that Jesus is the Christ, the Son of the living God (6:69).

Jesus then says that He has chosen the Twelve, yet one of them is a devil; the passage identifies Judas as the one who would betray Him (6:70–71).

This ending reinforces the need to distinguish outward association, appointment among the Twelve, believing confession, and the Father's giving. The passage records all these categories but does not authorize us to flatten them into one label.

**Finding from the whole discourse:** John 6 does not hide departure or unbelief. It places them beside the Father's giving, drawing, teaching, and granting, and beside the Son's promise to receive, lose nothing, and raise those given. The departure of some hearers does not state that the Son has lost one of those the Father gave Him.

## 6. John 10:22–30 — the sheep are identified by Christ

### 6.1 The stated reason for unbelief — verse 26

Jesus says:

> But ye believe not, because ye are not of my sheep, as I said unto you. (John 10:26, KJV)

The causal direction must not be reversed. Jesus does not say in this sentence that they are not His sheep because they first failed to believe. He says they do not believe because they are not of His sheep.

This is a direct statement in the immediate controversy. It must be retained without using it to pronounce the final state of every person in a later church or every person who has ever heard a warning.

### 6.2 The sheep's hearing and following — verse 27

Jesus says:

> My sheep hear my voice, and I know them, and they follow me:

Hearing and following are not erased by divine initiative. They are the stated marks of the sheep in this passage. Christ knows them; their response is real.

The text does not say that their following independently causes the Father to give them to the Son. John 10:29 explicitly says the Father gave them to the Son.

### 6.3 The promise — verses 28–30

Jesus says:

- “I give unto them eternal life.”
- “They shall never perish.”
- “Neither shall any man pluck them out of my hand.”
- The Father gave them to the Son.
- No one is able to pluck them out of the Father's hand.
- “I and my Father are one.”

The promise is not weak or uncertain. Christ is the giver of eternal life, and His sheep are secure in His and the Father's hand.

### 6.4 What the passage does not say

John 10:27–29 does not use the terms “candlestick,” “crown,” “Jezebel,” “second death,” or “overcomer.” It does not explain the exact relation between every warning in Revelation and every aspect of the Son's preserving work.

It also does not say that the sheep's hearing and following are optional. Those verbs are part of Christ's description of His sheep.

**Finding:** The sheep are not defined by mere proximity to a religious community. Christ knows them; they hear and follow Him. The Father has given them to the Son; the Son gives them eternal life and promises that they will never perish and cannot be snatched from His or the Father's hand.

## 7. The key distinction: the visible church and Christ's sheep

The messages in Revelation are addressed to actual congregations. John 10 describes Christ's sheep. The texts are related, but they do not expressly say that every person whose name appears on a local congregation's membership list is one of the sheep described in John 10.

The New Testament itself gives reasons to preserve this distinction:

1. In John 6, many people are called disciples, yet some do not believe and many turn back (6:64–66).
2. In John 10, Jesus distinguishes those who do not believe because they are not His sheep from the sheep who hear and follow (10:26–27).
3. In Revelation 2, Pergamos and Thyatira contain distinct groups: some hold corrupt teaching, while others hold fast or do not hold that doctrine (2:14–16; 2:20–25).
4. In Revelation 3, Sardis has a reputation of life, yet Christ says it is dead; He also recognizes a few who have not defiled their garments (3:1–4).
5. In Revelation 3, Laodicea's self-assessment is contradicted by Christ's assessment (3:17–19).

**Supported inference:** A visible church can contain differing spiritual conditions and responses. Church association, reputation, and outward proximity do not by themselves establish that every person is one of Christ's sheep.

**Boundary:** This distinction must not be used to make the warnings irrelevant to believers. Christ addresses the churches and commands them to hear, repent, and hold fast. Nor does this investigation identify the final state of every individual in those congregations.

## 8. Do the warnings have real force?

Yes. They are not decorative, empty, or to be dismissed as though Christ did not mean what He said.

The messages announce real consequences:

- Ephesus: removal of the candlestick if it does not repent (2:5).
- Smyrna: real persecution, imprisonment, and the call to faithfulness unto death (2:10).
- Pergamos: Christ will fight against those who persist in the condemned teaching (2:16).
- Thyatira: judgment upon the unrepentant teacher and those following her, and recompense according to works (2:21–23).
- Sardis: Christ will come as a thief if they do not watch (3:3).
- Philadelphia: the command to hold fast so that no one take its crown (3:11).
- Laodicea: the warning that Christ will spue the lukewarm church out of His mouth (3:16), together with His rebuke, chastening, and call to repent (3:19).

The warnings have different objects and consequences. They must not all be collapsed into one statement about eternal condemnation.

### 8.1 Corporate consequence is not automatically an individual salvation verdict

The removal of a candlestick is addressed to a church. It is a grave warning about that church's place as a lampstand. The text does not say that every person in that congregation is thereby eternally lost.

Likewise, the command to hold fast and the warning concerning a crown in Philadelphia must be taken seriously, but the text does not define the crown as a technical synonym for eternal life or say that its loss is identical to being plucked from Christ's hand.

The second death, by contrast, is explicitly named in Smyrna's promise (2:11) and later in Revelation 20–21. The text's own distinctions must be preserved.

### 8.2 Chastening is not identical to final rejection

Laodicea is the clearest example within the seven messages: Christ says He rebukes and chastens those He loves and commands repentance (3:19). A warning can be an expression of Christ's corrective dealings.

But this does not allow us to reduce every warning to chastening. Thyatira includes announced judgment upon the unrepentant, and Ephesus faces the removal of its candlestick. Both corrective and judicial dimensions remain.

**Finding:** The warnings are real words from the risen Christ, addressed to real churches and people. They call for real response and announce real consequences. Their precise consequence must be read from each passage, not assumed to be identical in every case.

## 9. How warnings and keeping stand together

The passages establish several complementary truths.

### 9.1 The Son's keeping is not a permission to ignore His voice

John 10 says the sheep hear Christ's voice and follow Him. It does not describe a sheep as one who refuses His voice while appealing to the promise of eternal life as a license for disobedience.

Revelation 2–3 repeatedly calls the churches to hear what the Spirit says. The call is consistent with Christ's description of His sheep.

### 9.2 The command to persevere does not make the creature the originating cause

The sheep's hearing and following are real, but John 10 identifies the Father as the giver and Christ as the giver of eternal life. John 6 identifies the Father as the one who gives, draws, teaches, and grants; the Son receives, keeps, loses nothing, and raises.

The command to hold fast does not transfer Original Cause from God to the creature. It identifies the response Christ requires.

### 9.3 The warning can expose false confidence and call for repentance

Sardis's reputation and Laodicea's self-assessment are both contradicted by Christ. Their warnings expose the difference between what a church says of itself and what Christ knows.

John 6 records outward discipleship without treating every outward follower as a believing sheep. John 10 distinguishes unbelievers from Christ's sheep. These passages together warn against using outward association as proof of saving belonging.

### 9.4 The warning and the promise address different conditions and referents

John 6:37–40 speaks of all the Father gives to the Son and the Son's mission not to lose them. John 10:27–29 speaks of Christ's sheep, whom He knows, who hear and follow, and whom He gives eternal life.

Revelation 2–3 addresses churches with mixed conditions, including faithful people, compromised people, people needing repentance, and churches whose self-assessment is false. It also speaks of corporate witness and consequences.

The texts are not contradictory simply because they do not address every subject in identical terms.

### 9.5 Warnings are among the means by which the churches are addressed and summoned

Revelation itself shows Christ speaking warnings, commands, promises, and calls to hear through the messages to the churches. Hebrews 3:12–14 provides a further canonical witness: believers are warned against an evil heart of unbelief and departing from the living God, and are told to exhort one another daily, while the passage also speaks of being made partakers of Christ if the beginning of confidence is held steadfast unto the end.

First Corinthians 10:1–13 similarly recounts Israel's failures as examples and warns the church: “Wherefore let him that thinketh he standeth take heed lest he fall” (10:12). The same passage says God is faithful and provides a way to escape temptation (10:13).

**Supported canonical inference:** Warnings are not opposed to God's preserving work. They are real instruments of instruction, exposure, exhortation, and summons within the life of the people addressed. The canon places warning and divine faithfulness in the same discourse.

**Boundary:** The passages do not give a complete metaphysical account of precisely how each warning relates to the Son's keeping of each person given by the Father. We should not turn the supported relationship into a fully specified mechanism that Scripture does not narrate.

## 10. The central synthesis

The following statements can all be retained without contradiction:

1. **The Father gives.** John 6:37, 39 and John 10:29 explicitly identify the Father as the giver of those who belong to the Son.
2. **Those given come.** John 6:37 says all that the Father gives shall come to the Son.
3. **The Son receives and keeps.** He will not cast out the one who comes, will lose nothing of all the Father has given Him, and will raise it at the last day (John 6:37–40).
4. **The sheep hear and follow.** Christ knows His sheep, and they hear His voice and follow Him (John 10:27).
5. **The Son gives eternal life.** His sheep shall never perish, and no one can snatch them from His or the Father's hand (John 10:28–29).
6. **Christ warns churches.** He commands repentance, watchfulness, faithfulness, and holding fast, and announces consequences where the churches persist in their failures (Revelation 2–3).
7. **The warnings must be heard.** The messages repeatedly call those with ears to hear what the Spirit says to the churches.
8. **Outward association is not identical to saving belonging.** John 6 and John 10 distinguish those who do not believe or continue from the sheep Christ knows; Revelation itself distinguishes differing conditions within the churches.
9. **The warnings do not state that the Son loses one of those the Father gave Him.** No passage examined here says that a person identified as one of the Father's given sheep is finally snatched from the Son's hand.
10. **The promises do not erase the warnings.** The Son's keeping is not permission to refuse His voice, and the churches are not told to disregard His commands.

**Integrated finding:** Christ's warnings and the Son's promises do not compete. The warnings address real churches and real conditions, expose false confidence and compromise, summon hearers to repentance and perseverance, and announce consequences. The promises in John 6 and John 10 declare the Father's giving and the Son's sure keeping of those who belong to Him. The sheep's hearing and following are real; they do not originate the Father's giving or the Son's saving work. The visible congregation and the sheep Christ knows must not be treated as automatically identical categories.

## 11. What is established, and what remains unresolved?

### Established by the passages examined

1. John 6:37–40 emphatically declares that all the Father gives come to the Son, the Son will not cast out the one who comes, He will lose nothing of what the Father gave Him, and He will raise it at the last day.
2. John 6:44–45 and 65 identify the Father's drawing, teaching, and granting in relation to coming to Christ.
3. John 6:60–66 records unbelief and the departure of many disciples in the same discourse as the promise of the Son's keeping.
4. John 6 does not expressly identify those who depart in verse 66 as the same persons given by the Father in verse 37.
5. John 10:26 states that certain hearers do not believe because they are not Christ's sheep.
6. John 10:27 identifies Christ's sheep by their hearing, being known by Him, and following Him.
7. John 10:28–29 declares that Christ gives His sheep eternal life, that they shall never perish, and that no one can snatch them from His or the Father's hand.
8. Revelation 2–3 contains real warnings, commands, calls to repentance, and promises.
9. The messages distinguish different conditions within several churches; the whole congregation is not described as having one undifferentiated response.
10. Philadelphia's promise that Christ will keep the church (Revelation 3:10) appears alongside the command to hold fast (3:11).
11. Laodicea's rebuke and chastening are explicitly connected with Christ's love and call to repentance (3:19).
12. Hebrews 3:12–14 and 1 Corinthians 10:1–13 place warnings, exhortation, perseverance, and divine faithfulness together.

### Supported inferences

1. The warnings of Revelation 2–3 are consistent with, rather than a refutation of, the Son's keeping in John 6 and John 10.
2. The warnings are real instruments of Christ's address, exposure, correction, and summons to the churches.
3. Outward church association and reputation cannot, by themselves, establish that every person is one of Christ's sheep.
4. The sheep's hearing and following are real marks of belonging, not independent originating causes of salvation.
5. The warnings can be taken with full seriousness without asserting that the Son loses those the Father has given Him.

### Not settled by this examination

- A complete account of the relation between every warning in Revelation and the final state of every individual addressed.
- Whether each warning is directed to a true believer, a false professor, a mixed congregation, or more than one category in every instance.
- A complete metaphysical or temporal mechanism explaining how the warnings function in relation to the Son's keeping.
- Whether “candlestick,” “crown,” “second death,” chastening, and final condemnation can be related in a single exhaustive scheme. The texts use distinct terms and contexts.
- The final state of every person who turned back in John 6:66.
- A complete chronology of the Father's giving, drawing, new birth, faith, perseverance, and final resurrection.
- An equation of every member of a visible church with the sheep of John 10, or of every overcomer phrase with one technical category in John.

These are bounded questions. They do not weaken the explicit promises or warnings.
