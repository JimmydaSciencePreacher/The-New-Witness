## 4. Revelation 13:8 — the Lamb, the Book, and the attachment question

The KJV reads:

> And all that dwell upon the earth shall worship him, whose names are not written in the book of life of the Lamb slain from the foundation of the world.

The immediate context is the beast's authority and the worship given to it by the earth-dwellers. The verse distinguishes those who worship the beast and refers to the Book of Life belonging to the Lamb.

### Textual observation

The Greek phrase translated “from the foundation of the world” follows the description of the Lamb as slain. Two grammatical attachments have therefore been read:

1. the phrase modifies the writing clause: the name has not been written in the Book of Life from the foundation of the world;
2. the phrase modifies the Lamb's being slain: the Lamb was slain from the foundation of the world.

The second reading is grammatically discussable because of the phrase's proximity to “the Lamb who was slain.” The first reading is also supported and is preferred by the NET translators, who point to Revelation 17:8 as an internal comparison where the same temporal phrase is clearly associated with the Book of Life writing clause.

The KJV's wording naturally presents the Lamb as slain from the foundation of the world. This investigation does not silently replace the KJV wording with another translation. It records the syntactic issue so that the verse is not made to bear more certainty than the grammar warrants.

The source reference work also records a textual-number distinction: the critical Greek text represented in its examination has “the name” in the clause, while the relative pronoun referring to the earth-dwellers is plural; later Greek forms have a plural “names.” This detail should remain a textual note, not become the basis of a doctrine.

**Finding:** Revelation 13:8 is relevant to the timing question, but its temporal phrase has a disputed attachment. It cannot stand alone as an undisputed proof that the Lamb was slain before Creation or that each name was positively inscribed at a specified pre-creation moment.

## 5. Revelation 17:8 — the clearest temporal statement

The KJV reads:

> The beast that thou sawest was, and is not; and shall ascend out of the bottomless pit, and go into perdition: and they that dwell on the earth shall wonder, whose names were not written in the book of life from the foundation of the world, when they behold the beast that was, and is not, and yet is.

The immediate context concerns the beast and the astonishment of the earth-dwellers when they see it. The Book of Life clause identifies the people who wonder after the beast.

### Textual observation

The critical Greek wording includes:

- **οὐ γέγραπται (ou gegraptai):** “has not been written” / “is not written.” The verb is perfect passive indicative, third-person singular, from the verb meaning “write.”
- **τὸ ὄνομα (to onoma):** “the name” in the critical Greek text examined in the source reference work. Some later textual forms read a plural “names.”
- **ἐπὶ τὸ βιβλίον τῆς ζωῆς (epi to biblion tēs zōēs):** “in/on the book of life.”
- **ἀπὸ καταβολῆς κόσμου (apo katabolēs kosmou):** “from/since the foundation of the world.”

Unlike Revelation 13:8, the temporal phrase in Revelation 17:8 follows the Book of Life writing clause and is naturally read with the status of being written. The NET textual note treats the attachment here as clear and uses it as an internal comparison for Revelation 13:8.

### What is explicit

John identifies a class of earth-dwellers whose names have not been written in the Book of Life from the foundation of the world. The non-inscription is placed in relation to the foundation of the world.

### What is not explicit

The verse does not narrate the positive act by which the names of the redeemed were written. It does not state a separate affirmative sentence giving the date or mechanics of that inscription. A negative statement about those not written must not be converted into a narrated positive event for those written.

The perfect form describes the written/not-written status in the clause. Its grammatical aspect does not, by itself, disclose a hidden mechanism or a calendar event.

### Finding

Revelation 17:8 supplies the clearest temporal testimony in the primary passages: the distinction concerning those not written in the Book of Life is related to the foundation of the world. It strongly places this distinction within God's world-spanning purpose. It does not disclose the exact positive inscription event or the precise moment at which each redeemed name was written.

## 6. Revelation 20:12–15 — the opened books, judgment, and the Book of Life

The KJV reads:

> 12 And I saw the dead, small and great, stand before God; and the books were opened: and another book was opened, which is the book of life: and the dead were judged out of those things which were written in the books, according to their works.  
> 13 And the sea gave up the dead which were in it; and death and hell delivered up the dead which were in them: and they were judged every man according to their works.  
> 14 And death and hell were cast into the lake of fire. This is the second death.  
> 15 And whosoever was not found written in the book of life was cast into the lake of fire.

### The passage's distinctions

The vision describes the dead standing before the throne. Books are opened; another book is opened, identified as the Book of Life. The dead are judged according to what is written in the books and according to their works. The sea, death, and hell give up the dead; death and hell are then cast into the lake of fire. Verse 15 states the consequence for the one not found written in the Book of Life.

The text distinguishes the plural books from the other book identified as the Book of Life. It also distinguishes judgment according to works from the stated finding concerning whether a person is written in the Book of Life.

### Textual observation

The Greek wording in verse 12 says that another book was opened, “which is of life.” The KJV renders this as “the book of life.” Verse 15 in Greek does not contain a separate noun corresponding to the KJV's supplied word “whosoever”; the English expresses the implied person. Likewise, the NET note observes that “name” is implied in verse 15 rather than explicitly present as a Greek noun. The central point remains: the one not found written in the Book of Life is cast into the lake of fire.

### What the passage establishes

- The Book of Life is opened in the judgment vision.
- The dead are judged according to their works as recorded in the books.
- The Book of Life is identified as a distinct book in the scene.
- Not being found written in the Book of Life has the stated consequence of being cast into the lake of fire.

### What the passage does not establish

It does not say when the Book of Life was first written, when any particular name was entered, or how the writing occurred. The opening of the book at the judgment scene is not a statement that the book was first created or first populated at that moment.

Nor should the passage be simplified into either of these unsupported claims: that works are irrelevant because the Book of Life is mentioned, or that the Book of Life is merely another record of works. The vision names both the books containing the deeds and the distinct Book of Life. Their full theological relation must be stated from the text and wider canon, not erased.

**Finding:** Revelation 20:12–15 establishes the Book of Life's role in the final judgment scene and the seriousness of not being found written. It supplies no inscription date or mechanics.

## 7. Revelation 21:27 — written in the Lamb's Book of Life and entry into the holy city

The KJV reads:

> And there shall in no wise enter into it any thing that defileth, neither whatsoever worketh abomination, or maketh a lie: but they which are written in the Lamb's book of life.

The immediate context is the New Jerusalem and the holiness of the new Creation. Nothing that defiles, practices abomination, or makes a lie enters. The exception identifies those written in the Lamb's Book of Life.

### Textual observation

The Greek expression **οἱ γεγραμμένοι (hoi gegrammenoi)** means “those written” or “those having been written.” It is a perfect passive participle. It identifies the people by their written status. It does not date the writing.

This passage expressly calls the register the Book of Life **of the Lamb**. It gives a strong canonical connection between the Book of Life and final entry into the holy city.

### Finding

Revelation 21:27 identifies those written in the Lamb's Book of Life as those admitted into the holy city. It confirms the book's association with belonging to the Lamb and the consummated new Creation. It does not specify when their names were written.

## 8. Luke 10:20 — names written in heaven

The KJV reads:

> Notwithstanding in this rejoice not, that the spirits are subject unto you; but rather rejoice, because your names are written in heaven.

The immediate context is the return of the appointed messengers, who report with joy that even the devils are subject to them through Jesus' name (Luke 10:17–19). Jesus redirects their rejoicing. Their authority over spirits is not to be their deepest ground of joy; they are to rejoice that their names are written in heaven.

### Textual observation

The Greek verb translated “are written” is a perfect form. The sentence presents their names as standing written in heaven. It does not state when that writing occurred.

Luke 10:20 does not explicitly use the title “Book of Life” or mention a physical book. It speaks of names written in heaven. The statement is closely related to the wider Scriptural testimony concerning heavenly registration, but the wording of Luke must remain distinct from Revelation's explicit references to the Book of Life.

### Finding

Luke 10:20 establishes that Jesus directs the messengers to rejoice in the heavenly written status of their names rather than in the authority exercised during their mission. It provides no date or mechanism for that writing. The verse also does not independently identify the precise moment when the Father gave these people to the Son.

## 9. Philippians 4:3 — fellow workers whose names are in the Book of Life

The KJV reads:

> And I intreat thee also, true yokefellow, help those women which laboured with me in the gospel, with Clement also, and with other my fellowlabourers, whose names are in the book of life.

The immediate context is Paul's appeal for help and reconciliation concerning fellow workers in the Gospel (Philippians 4:2–3). He names women who laboured with him, Clement, and other fellow labourers, and describes the group as people whose names are in the Book of Life.

### Textual observation

The Greek clause says, literally, “whose names in [the] book of life”; the verb “are” is supplied in English to express the nominal clause. The verse identifies the fellow workers' names as belonging in the Book of Life. It does not say when they were written.

The verse places the Book of Life reference within Paul's ordinary pastoral instruction and recognition of fellow labourers. Their Gospel labour is real and is named. The statement does not say that their labour caused their names to be written; neither does the verse provide an inscription chronology.

### Finding

Philippians 4:3 confirms the Book of Life as a real category in Paul's language for those who belong to the Lord. It identifies fellow workers as having their names in the book but supplies no date or mechanism for inscription.

## 10. The Father's giving and the Book of Life — relationship without equation

The prior source investigations establish the following testimony:

### The Father's giving

- **John 6:37–40:** the Father gives to the Son; those given come; the Son receives them, loses none of what the Father has given, and raises them at the last day. The passage also speaks of seeing the Son and believing.
- **John 10:27–29:** the sheep hear the Son's voice and follow Him; He knows them and gives them eternal life. The Father gave them to the Son, and no one can pluck them from the Son's or the Father's hand.
- **John 17:2, 6, 9, 24:** Jesus speaks of the authority given Him, the people given to Him by the Father, His keeping of them, and His desire that they be with Him.

The source investigation has already established that the Father gives and the Son receives and keeps. Those given come; the sheep hear and follow; believers truly believe. These human responses are real and remain within the Father's purpose.

### The temporal boundary in John 17

John 17:24 speaks of those whom the Father has given to the Son and then says that the Father loved the Son before the foundation of the world. The temporal phrase directly qualifies the Father's love for the Son. It must not be moved grammatically so that it dates the giving of the disciples.

John 17:6 uses the language of giving; the form of the verb does not itself establish a pre-creation date. John 6:37 likewise states that the Father gives, but does not date that giving to a point before the foundation of the world.

### The Book of Life testimony

Revelation 17:8 relates the non-inscription of certain names to the foundation of the world. Revelation 21:27 identifies those written in the Lamb's Book of Life as those who enter the holy city. Revelation 20:12–15 places the Book of Life in the final judgment scene. Luke 10:20 speaks of names written in heaven, and Philippians 4:3 identifies fellow workers as having names in the Book of Life.

### The canonical relationship

The passages belong together in the wider Scriptural account of the Father's purpose, the Son's saving work, those who belong to Him, and the final judgment and consummation. There is a strong canonical correspondence between the people the Father gives to the Son, those the Son keeps and gives life, and those identified as written in the Lamb's Book of Life.

However, the six primary passages do not contain a sentence that explicitly says, “Everyone given by the Father to the Son is the same list of names written in the Book of Life,” nor do they narrate the moment at which that list was inscribed. The correspondence is a supported canonical relationship; a precise identity statement or mechanism would go beyond the wording examined.

**Finding:** The Father's giving and the Book of Life are related testimonies concerning those who belong to the Son/Lamb. Scripture does not authorize the Witness to collapse them into one technical event or to assign them an identical timestamp without further explicit evidence.

## 11. Foreknowledge and the foundation of the world — what the related passages date

The primary Book of Life passages must be compared with the texts that expressly speak of God's prior purpose:

- **Romans 8:28–30:** God foreknows, predestines, calls, justifies, and glorifies those described in the passage. The passage establishes God's agency and the unity of the people carried through the stated sequence. It does not itself use the phrase “before the foundation of the world.”
- **Ephesians 1:3–14:** God chose in Christ before the foundation of the world and predestinated according to the good pleasure of His will. The passage also speaks of redemption through Christ's blood, the Gospel being heard, believing, and the sealing of the Holy Spirit. The explicit pre-foundation phrase dates the choosing stated in verse 4.
- **1 Peter 1:1–5, 18–21:** believers are elect according to the foreknowledge of God the Father; Christ was foreordained before the foundation of the world and manifested in the last times. Peter distinguishes prior divine purpose from historical manifestation and retains the Spirit's sanctifying work, obedience, faith, and the Word.
- **2 Timothy 1:9:** God's purpose and grace in Christ Jesus are placed before the world began and are now manifested through Christ's appearing.
- **Titus 1:2:** the promise of eternal life is placed before the world began and is manifested in due times through preaching.

These passages establish prior divine purpose, choosing, foreordination, grace, and promise. They do not state that the phrase “foreknown” is itself another name for “written in the Book of Life.” Nor does a passage that dates election or foreordination automatically narrate the exact positive inscription event.

The source's prior foreknowledge investigation also preserves the semantic question concerning the full force of foreknowledge in Romans 8:29 and 1 Peter 1:2. This investigation does not settle that question by redefining foreknowledge as inscription.

**Finding:** Scripture explicitly places important aspects of God's saving purpose before the foundation of the world or before the ages. That prior purpose coheres with the Book of Life testimony. The exact moment and mechanics of inscription remain unspecified.

## 12. Integrated answer — does Scripture establish when the names were written?

The evidence supports these conclusions, with their limits:

1. **A foundation-of-the-world horizon is explicit for non-inscription.** Revelation 17:8 most clearly states that the names of a certain class of earth-dwellers were not written in the Book of Life from the foundation of the world.
2. **Revelation 13:8 is relevant but syntactically disputed.** Its temporal phrase may be read with the Book of Life writing clause or with the Lamb's being slain. Revelation 17:8 is the clearer internal comparison.
3. **The positive inscription event is not narrated.** The passages identify people as written, not written, or found written. They do not provide a separate scene, exact date, or mechanics for the inscription of each redeemed person's name.
4. **The Father's giving is explicit, but its precise temporal point is not.** John 6, John 10, and John 17 establish the giving and the Son's keeping. They do not independently date the giving before the foundation of the world.
5. **Prior divine purpose is explicit in other passages.** Ephesians 1:4–5, 1 Peter 1:19–20, 2 Timothy 1:9, and Titus 1:2 expressly place choosing, Christ's foreordination, grace, or promise before the foundation/world began.
6. **The Book of Life has a clear final function.** Revelation 20:12–15 places it in the judgment scene; Revelation 21:27 identifies those written in the Lamb's Book of Life as those who enter the holy city.
7. **The canonical relationship is real but must not be overdefined.** The Father's giving, the Son's keeping, prior election and purpose, and the Book of Life concern the saving identity and destiny of those who belong to the Lamb. The passages examined do not explicitly equate all these expressions as one event or disclose the inscription mechanism.

**Answer:** Scripture establishes that the Book of Life distinction reaches back to the foundation-of-the-world horizon, most clearly through Revelation 17:8's statement about those not written. Scripture also establishes the Father's prior purpose and His giving of people to the Son. But Scripture does not separately narrate the exact positive moment or mechanics by which each redeemed person's name was written, nor does it date the Father's giving in John 6 and John 17 to that same moment. We must not manufacture a timestamp.
