### 3.3 What am I learning about what I am witnessing?

#### A. Romans 8:24–25 — saved in hope, waiting for what is not seen

Paul writes:

> For we are saved by hope: but hope that is seen is not hope: for what a man seeth, why doth he yet hope for?  
> But if we hope for that we see not, then do we with patience wait for it.

**TEXT STATES:**

- Paul describes believers as saved by hope.
- Hope concerns what is not yet seen.
- The unseen object of hope is awaited with patience.

**CONTEXT:** Romans 8:18–23 has just described present suffering, Creation's expectation, and the redemption of the body. Therefore, the hope in verses 24–25 is not vague optimism. It belongs to Paul's argument about future glory, adoption, bodily redemption, and Creation's promised deliverance.

**RECOGNIZED RELATIONSHIP:** The promise is real even though its full manifestation is not yet seen. Waiting does not make the promise its own source; it is the creature's patient expectation of what God has promised.

**BOUNDARY:** “Saved by hope” must not be detached from Paul's wider teaching about salvation by grace, faith, Christ's work, and the Spirit. Nor does the phrase mean that subjective optimism saves. Paul is describing the orientation of those who await the promised consummation.

#### B. Romans 8:26 — the Spirit helps our infirmities

Paul continues:

> Likewise the Spirit also helpeth our infirmities: for we know not what we should pray for as we ought: but the Spirit itself maketh intercession for us with groanings which cannot be uttered.

**TEXT STATES:**

- The Spirit helps believers in their infirmities.
- Believers do not know what they should pray for as they ought.
- The Spirit makes intercession for them.
- This intercession is described with “groanings which cannot be uttered.”

**IMPORTANT DISTINCTION — THREE GROANINGS:**

| Passage | Subject | Statement |
|---|---|---|
| Romans 8:22 | The whole creation | Groans and travails together. |
| Romans 8:23 | Believers | Groan within themselves while waiting for adoption and bodily redemption. |
| Romans 8:26 | The Spirit | Helps believers' infirmities and makes intercession with groanings which cannot be uttered. |

These statements belong to the same argument, but they are not interchangeable. Paul changes the subject and describes distinct forms of groaning.

**RECOGNIZED RELATIONSHIP:** The believer's weakness in prayer does not mean the Spirit is absent or unable to help. The Spirit's intercession is God's provision, not a humanly generated substitute for divine action.

**BOUNDARY — “THE SPIRIT ITSELF”:** The KJV uses “itself” in this verse. The Greek noun for Spirit is grammatically neuter; English pronoun choice should not be used to deny the personal agency attributed to the Spirit in the passage. The verb describes the Spirit's intercession. This verse must be read alongside the wider Scriptural witness concerning the Spirit.

**BOUNDARY — UNUTTERABLE GROANINGS:** Paul does not say here that believers must produce a particular vocal experience, nor does the verse by itself define the phenomenon as a specific modern practice. The text says the Spirit intercedes with groanings which cannot be uttered. Do not turn the wording into a mechanism or practice the verse does not specify.

#### C. Romans 8:27 — God searches the hearts

Paul writes:

> And he that searcheth the hearts knoweth what is the mind of the Spirit, because he maketh intercession for the saints according to the will of God.

**TEXT STATES:**

- God searches the hearts.
- He knows the mind of the Spirit.
- The Spirit intercedes for the saints.
- The intercession is according to the will of God.

**RECOGNIZED RELATIONSHIP:** The Spirit's intercession is not independent of God's will. The verse explicitly joins the Spirit's intercession and the will of God. The believer's inability to know how to pray as one ought is not a limitation upon God's knowledge or purpose.

**BOUNDARY:** Do not make this verse say that the Spirit and the Father are the same person, or use it to erase the distinctions present in the text. The passage describes their relationship in the work of intercession; it is not a complete technical exposition of the Trinity.

#### D. Romans 8:28 — all things work together for good according to purpose

The KJV reads:

> And we know that all things work together for good to them that love God, to them who are the called according to his purpose.

**TEXT STATES:**

- Paul says “we know.”
- The statement concerns those who love God.
- They are identified as the called according to His purpose.
- All things work together for good for them.

**CONTEXT:** The verse follows the Spirit's intercession and precedes Paul's description of foreknowledge, predestination, calling, justification, and glorification. It must not be detached from that sequence or reduced to the claim that every event is itself good.

**RECOGNIZED RELATIONSHIP:** The good in view is governed by God's purpose, which the next verse identifies in relation to conformity to the image of His Son. The passage does not define “good” as immediate comfort, wealth, health, or the absence of suffering. Romans 8:18 has already acknowledged present suffering.

**TEXTUAL NOTE:** There is a well-known textual and translation question concerning the subject and wording of Romans 8:28. The KJV reads “all things work together for good”; some translations render the sense as God working all things for good. This investigation retains the KJV as its base and does not make the entire theological conclusion depend upon resolving that textual question. The context explicitly identifies God's purpose and action in the surrounding verses.

#### E. Romans 8:29 — foreknown and predestinated to conformity to the Son

Paul writes:

> For whom he did foreknow, he also did predestinate to be conformed to the image of his Son, that he might be the firstborn among many brethren.

**TEXT STATES:**

- God foreknew those in view.
- Those foreknown are also predestinated.
- The stated destination is conformity to the image of His Son.
- The stated purpose includes that the Son might be the firstborn among many brethren.

**RECOGNIZED RELATIONSHIP:** Predestination is not presented here as an abstract label without an identified end. Paul explicitly states the end: conformity to the image of God's Son. The purpose is Christ-centered.

**BOUNDARY — FOREKNOW:** The verse uses “foreknow,” but does not pause to define the term in a lexical treatise. The wider canonical record contains God's prior purpose, election in Christ, and foreordination of the Son. The term must be examined in context and cannot responsibly be reduced to a simplistic slogan. This investigation records the relationship without pretending that one English word alone settles every debate about foreknowledge.

**BOUNDARY — CONFORMITY:** The verse states the purpose of conformity to the Son's image. It does not say that believers become the Son, become divine in identity, or become the Author. The Son remains the firstborn among many brethren; believers are those conformed to His image.

#### F. Romans 8:30 — the sequence of predestination, calling, justification, and glorification

Paul writes:

> Moreover whom he did predestinate, them he also called: and whom he called, them he also justified: and whom he justified, them he also glorified.

**TEXT STATES:** Paul gives a connected sequence:
1. Predestinated.
2. Called.
3. Justified.
4. Glorified.

The repeated “whom” connects the groups through the sequence. The passage presents God's purpose as effective and certain in Paul's argument.

**RECOGNIZED RELATIONSHIP:** The calling and justification of those in view are not presented as independent human authorship. The sequence begins with God's purpose and ends with glorification.

**BOUNDARY — “GLORIFIED”:** Paul uses the past-tense form “glorified” while discussing the future glory anticipated in Romans 8:18 and the bodily redemption awaited in verse 23. The verse is commonly understood as expressing the certainty of God's completed purpose, sometimes described as a proleptic use. This does not mean that every believer's bodily glorification has already occurred in the ordinary historical sense. Preserve Paul's wording and the future hope in the same chapter.

**BOUNDARY — SCOPE:** Romans 8:30 is part of Paul's argument in Romans 8. It must be read with the chapter and the wider letter, not turned into an independent chart that answers every question about the timing or subjective experience of every stage of salvation.

#### G. 1 Corinthians 15:1–11 — the Gospel Paul received and delivered

Paul begins by reminding the Corinthians of the Gospel he preached, which they received and in which they stand. He states that Christ died for our sins according to the Scriptures, was buried, and rose again the third day according to the Scriptures. He then lists appearances to Cephas, the twelve, more than five hundred brethren at once, James, all the apostles, and finally Paul.

**TEXT STATES:**

- The Gospel is received and stood in.
- Christ died for our sins.
- He was buried.
- He rose again the third day.
- The Resurrection is supported in the chapter by named witnesses and appearances.

**RECOGNIZED RELATIONSHIP:** Paul's teaching about the future resurrection is grounded in the proclamation that Christ has already risen. The Resurrection is not a detached illustration or an optional addition to the Gospel.

**BOUNDARY:** The appearance list in verses 5–8 is Paul's own summary. It must be compared with the Gospel accounts and Acts without forcing every list to be an identical transcript or inventing a complete chronology from this paragraph alone.

#### H. 1 Corinthians 15:12–19 — if there is no resurrection

Paul confronts those who say there is no resurrection of the dead. He argues that if the dead rise not, Christ is not risen; if Christ is not risen, apostolic preaching and faith are vain, the apostles are false witnesses, believers remain in their sins, and those fallen asleep in Christ have perished.

**TEXT STATES:** The Resurrection of Christ and the future resurrection of the dead are joined in Paul's argument. Denying the resurrection of the dead undermines the Gospel's claim that Christ is risen and the hope of believers.

**RECOGNIZED RELATIONSHIP:** The future resurrection is not a secondary doctrine that can be removed without consequence. It is integral to Paul's Gospel reasoning.

#### I. 1 Corinthians 15:20–28 — Christ the firstfruits

Paul declares:

> But now is Christ risen from the dead, and become the firstfruits of them that slept.

He then gives the order:
- By man came death.
- By man also comes the resurrection of the dead.
- In Adam all die.
- In Christ shall all be made alive.
- Every man in his own order: Christ the firstfruits; afterward they that are Christ's at His coming.
- Then comes the end, when Christ delivers the kingdom to God, even the Father, after putting down all rule, authority, and power.
- He must reign till all enemies are under His feet.
- The last enemy that shall be destroyed is death.

**TEXT STATES:** Christ is risen and is the firstfruits. Paul connects the first Adam with death and Christ with resurrection. He identifies an order: Christ first, then those who belong to Him at His coming.

**RECOGNIZED RELATIONSHIP:** Firstfruits is not simply a title of honor detached from the harvest. In Paul's argument it identifies Christ's Resurrection as the beginning and guarantee of the coming resurrection of those who belong to Him. The harvest language must remain connected to Paul's stated order.

**BOUNDARY — “IN ADAM” AND “IN CHRIST”:** The scope of “all” in verses 22–23 must be interpreted with the immediate context and the chapter's order. Verse 23 specifically identifies “they that are Christ's” as raised at His coming. Do not use verse 22 alone to erase Paul's distinctions or to assert a universal outcome that the passage's argument does not establish.

**BOUNDARY — FIRSTFRUITS:** Christ is not merely the first person ever restored to life in Scripture. Paul identifies His Resurrection as firstfruits in a distinctive order of resurrection and contrasts it with the death that came through Adam. The raisings performed during Jesus' earthly ministry and the Resurrection of Jesus must not be collapsed into the same category.

#### J. 1 Corinthians 15:35–49 — the natural body, the spiritual body, and the two Adams

Paul answers questions about how the dead are raised and with what body they come. He uses the image of sowing and distinguishes:
- corruption and incorruption;
- dishonour and glory;
- weakness and power;
- a natural body and a spiritual body.

He then quotes Genesis concerning the first man Adam becoming a living soul and says the last Adam was made a quickening spirit. He distinguishes the first man, who is of the earth, earthy, from the second man, the Lord from heaven. Those who have borne the image of the earthy shall also bear the image of the heavenly.

**TEXT STATES:**

- The body that is raised is not described as identical in condition to the body sown.
- The natural body and spiritual body are distinguished.
- The first Adam is associated with the natural and earthy.
- The last Adam is associated with life-giving and the heavenly.
- Those who bear the image of the earthy shall bear the image of the heavenly.

**RECOGNIZED RELATIONSHIP:** Paul's account of conformity to the Son's image in Romans 8:29 coheres with his account in 1 Corinthians 15 of bearing the image of the heavenly. The passages are not identical in wording, but both place the believer's future in relation to Christ.

**BOUNDARY — SPIRITUAL BODY:** “Spiritual body” does not mean a body that is unreal, merely symbolic, or nonbodily. Paul's argument concerns resurrection of the dead and the body that is raised. The contrast is about the body's condition and governing life, not the disappearance of embodiment.

**BOUNDARY — LAST ADAM:** Christ is not a revised version of the first Adam or a creaturely stage in a system. Paul identifies Him as the last Adam in the context of the source of life and the resurrection order. GUA-W must not turn Christ into a component of its architecture.

#### K. 1 Corinthians 15:50–58 — incorruption and victory over death

Paul states that flesh and blood cannot inherit the kingdom of God, neither does corruption inherit incorruption. He then reveals a mystery: not all shall sleep, but all shall be changed, in a moment, at the last trump. The dead shall be raised incorruptible, and the living shall be changed. This corruptible must put on incorruption, and this mortal must put on immortality.

Paul concludes by declaring the saying fulfilled that death is swallowed up in victory, and asks, “O death, where is thy sting? O grave, where is thy victory?” He then gives thanks to God, who gives the victory through Jesus Christ our Lord, and exhorts believers to be steadfast and abound in the work of the Lord.

**TEXT STATES:** The future includes transformation, incorruption, immortality, and victory over death through Jesus Christ.

**RECOGNIZED RELATIONSHIP:** Romans 8's awaited redemption of the body and conformity to the Son are illuminated by 1 Corinthians 15's explicit resurrection teaching. The final hope is bodily transformation, not the abandonment of the body.

**BOUNDARY:** Paul does not provide a laboratory description of the transformation, nor a complete physical account of the risen body's properties. The text's theological claims must be preserved without inventing a mechanism.

#### L. The Father's purpose, the Son's firstfruits, and the Spirit's work — related but distinct witnesses

The passages examined can be held together without flattening their distinct statements:

- **The Father:** Ephesians 1:3–14 speaks of His purpose, will, election, adoption, and redemption in Christ. Romans 8:28–30 speaks of those called according to His purpose and the sequence leading to glorification.
- **The Son:** Christ is the Word made flesh; He dies, is buried, and is raised. He is the firstfruits of those who sleep and the last Adam.
- **The Spirit:** Romans 8:11 relates the Spirit to the raising and life of believers' mortal bodies; Romans 8:26–27 describes the Spirit's help and intercession.
- **The believer:** hears, believes, loves God, waits in hope, suffers, prays in weakness, and is promised conformity to the Son and bodily redemption.

The distinctions must remain. The Father, Son, and Spirit are not competing causes or interchangeable labels. Nor does this investigation attempt to produce a complete systematic exposition of the Trinity from these passages alone.

## 7. Conclusion within scope

Romans 8:24–30 presents believers waiting in hope for what is not yet seen. The Spirit helps their infirmities and intercedes according to the will of God. Paul then identifies those called according to God's purpose and states the end: conformity to the image of His Son. The sequence of predestination, calling, justification, and glorification is presented as God's purposeful action.

First Corinthians 15 grounds the hope of resurrection in the historical and apostolic proclamation that Christ died, was buried, and rose again. Paul identifies Christ as the firstfruits of those who sleep and the last Adam. Those who belong to Christ are raised at His coming. The chapter describes the raising of the dead in incorruption and glory, culminating in the destruction of death and victory through Jesus Christ.

The two passages are coherent witnesses to one Christ-centered hope: God's purpose, the Spirit's help, conformity to the Son, and the resurrection of those who belong to Christ. This relationship is already recognized in the GUA-W source repository; this investigation records its textual details and boundaries.

**The central recognition is:**

**The Father's purpose is His own. The Spirit helps and intercedes according to God's will. The Son is risen, the firstfruits, and the last Adam. Those who belong to Christ await the resurrection and conformity to His image.**

This conclusion does not make the Son a component of GUA-W, invent a mechanism for the Spirit's intercession or the resurrection body, or claim that the future glorification has already appeared in its full historical manifestation.
