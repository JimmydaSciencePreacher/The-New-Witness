### 3.3 What am I learning about what I am witnessing?

#### A. Genesis 3:8–11 — God calls and questions

Genesis 3:8 records the man and woman hearing the voice of the LORD God walking in the garden in the cool of the day. They hide themselves among the trees.

Genesis 3:9 then records:

> And the LORD God called unto Adam, and said unto him, Where art thou?

The direction of the encounter is explicit: the LORD God calls; Adam is the one addressed.

Genesis 3:10 records Adam's answer: he heard God's voice, was afraid because he was naked, and hid himself. In verse 11 God asks who told him he was naked and whether he had eaten from the tree from which He had commanded him not to eat.

**TEXT STATES:** God calls, asks, and directs the exchange toward the human condition and the prior command. Adam reports hearing, fear, nakedness, and hiding.

**SUPPORTED INFERENCE:** The question “Where art thou?” functions in the narrative as God's initiating call and summons into an encounter. It is not necessary to read it as a request for information God lacks. The source reconstruction likewise rejects the idea that God is seeking information for His own correction.

**BOUNDARY:** The text does not give a complete account of every divine attribute or explain the mechanics of divine knowledge in these verses. The safe conclusion is narrower: the question does not require the assumption that God is ignorant of Adam's location.

The relationship to GUA-W is direct: the Author initiates the encounter. The creature's hiding does not remove the creature from the Author's presence or authority.

#### B. Genesis 3:12–13 — the human answers and attributes

Adam answers in verse 12:

> The woman whom thou gavest to be with me, she gave me of the tree, and I did eat.

The answer contains several elements: reference to the woman, reference to God's giving of the woman, reference to her giving the fruit, and Adam's admission, “I did eat.”

Eve answers in verse 13:

> The serpent beguiled me, and I did eat.

Her answer identifies the serpent's beguiling and her own eating.

**TEXT STATES:** Adam and Eve each identify another party's influence or action, and each also acknowledges eating.

**TEXTUAL OBSERVATION:** Adam's answer points to the woman and to God's gift. Eve's answer points to the serpent's deception. Neither answer removes the action each has acknowledged.

**SUPPORTED INFERENCE:** Their answers reveal attribution and displacement of attention, but the text does not require us to infer that every part of either answer is false. Adam's statement that the woman gave him the fruit and Eve's statement that the serpent beguiled her are part of the recorded account. The issue is that those statements do not erase their own participation.

**BOUNDARY:** Do not claim that deception erased human agency; do not claim that human agency makes deception unreal. The passage retains both.

#### C. Genesis 3:14–19 — God identifies and judges

God addresses the serpent in verses 14–15, the woman in verse 16, and Adam in verses 17–19. The judgments are not authored by the humans or by the serpent. God speaks and assigns the consequences.

**The serpent — Genesis 3:14–15**

Verse 14 pronounces the serpent cursed above cattle and every beast of the field, with the stated condition of going upon its belly and eating dust all the days of its life.

Verse 15 declares enmity between the serpent and the woman, and between their respective seed. It then describes the bruising of the head and heel.

**TEXT STATES:** God says, “I will put enmity.” The initiative belongs to God. The verse establishes conflict and describes the injuries in its own terms.

**CANONICAL BOUNDARY:** The KJV verse does not name Jesus or narrate the later victory in full. The GUA-W source reconstruction separately examines Revelation 12, Romans 16:20, Hebrews 2:14–15, 1 John 3:8, Colossians 2:14–15, Romans 5, 1 Corinthians 15, and Galatians 3:16. Its careful distinction is retained: the New Testament gives a Christ-centered account of victory over Satan, while individual passages must not be misrepresented as direct quotations or grammatical explanations of Genesis 3:15 when they are not.

The Garden does not repeat that full canonical investigation here. It records that Genesis 3:15 is a divine declaration, not a human recovery plan.

**The woman — Genesis 3:16**

Verse 16 addresses multiplied sorrow/conception and childbirth, and the woman's relation to her husband, including the statement that he shall rule over her.

**TEXT STATES:** This is a divine pronouncement in the aftermath of the transgression.

**BOUNDARY:** Do not convert the verse into a blanket authorization for every form of human domination or abuse. Equally, do not erase the actual words of the passage. The nature, scope, and canonical treatment of this statement require careful contextual study; this investigation does not settle every theological and pastoral question surrounding it.

**Adam — Genesis 3:17–19**

Verse 17 identifies Adam's hearkening to his wife's voice and eating from the tree God had commanded him not to eat. The ground is cursed for his sake. Verses 18–19 describe thorns, thistles, labor, bread, sweat, and the return to the ground from which man was taken.

**TEXT STATES:** God directly identifies Adam's hearkening and eating in connection with the judgment. The consequences extend to the ground and to the conditions of human labor and mortality.

**RECOGNIZED RELATIONSHIP:** The human will acts, but it does not author the standard or determine the judgment. God identifies the action and speaks the consequence.

**BOUNDARY:** These verses do not establish that every instance of suffering, difficulty in childbirth, marital conflict, or agricultural hardship can be mapped one-to-one to a particular person's personal sin.

#### D. Genesis 3:20–21 — Adam names; God clothes

Genesis 3:20 records Adam calling his wife's name Eve, with the stated reason that she was the mother of all living.

Genesis 3:21 records:

> Unto Adam also and to his wife did the LORD God make coats of skins, and clothed them.

Two distinct actions are recorded:

- Adam names his wife.
- God makes the coats of skins and clothes them.

**TEXT STATES:** The provision of clothing is explicitly attributed to the LORD God.

**RECOGNIZED RELATIONSHIP:** Human naming and divine provision must not be collapsed. Adam's naming is a human act within the narrative; the clothing is God's act. The Author remains the provider even after the transgression.

**BOUNDARY:** Genesis 3:21 does not explicitly name an animal, describe a sacrifice, or explain a ritual mechanism. A later theological interpretation may examine possible canonical correspondences, but it must not present an unstated sacrifice as though the verse itself narrated one.

This distinction matters for the Witness: do not add details to a source simply because they fit a familiar theological explanation.

#### E. Genesis 3:22–24 — the Tree of Life and guarded access

Genesis 3:22 records the LORD God speaking of the man having become “as one of us, to know good and evil,” and states the concern that he might take also of the Tree of Life, eat, and live for ever.

Genesis 3:23 records the LORD God sending the man forth from the Garden of Eden to till the ground from which he was taken.

Genesis 3:24 records that God drove out the man and placed cherubims and a flaming sword east of the Garden to keep the way of the Tree of Life.

**TEXT STATES:**

- God identifies the concern about the man taking from the Tree of Life and living for ever.
- God sends the man out of the Garden.
- God places the cherubims and flaming sword to keep the way of the Tree of Life.

**RECOGNIZED RELATIONSHIP:** Access to life is not independently seized or secured by the creature. The passage places the boundary and its enforcement under God's action. The human does not become the Author or sovereign governor of life through the transgression.

**SUPPORTED INFERENCE:** The guarded way prevents the man from taking and eating from the Tree of Life in the condition described in verse 22. The text explicitly identifies this as the stated concern and the reason connected with the expulsion and guarding.

**BOUNDARY:** Do not invent a biological mechanism for the Tree of Life or claim that the man acquired God's authority. The phrase “as one of us” must be read in its immediate and wider canonical context; it does not state that man became God.

The source reconstruction notes that Revelation 21–22 returns to the Tree of Life in the consummation. That canonical relationship deserves its own focused study rather than being compressed into a claim that every detail of Genesis 3:22–24 is already explained by a single later image.

#### F. The whole movement of Genesis 3:8–24

The passage records a sequence:

1. The humans hear and hide.
2. God calls and questions.
3. Adam answers, identifying fear, nakedness, and hiding.
4. God directs attention to the prior command and the act.
5. Adam and Eve answer, each identifying another influence and each acknowledging eating.
6. God addresses the serpent, the woman, and the man.
7. Adam names his wife.
8. God makes clothing and clothes them.
9. God sends the man out.
10. God guards the way to the Tree of Life.

The passage preserves the distinction between the human acts and God's actions. The humans hear, hide, answer, attribute, and name. God calls, questions, identifies, judges, provides, sends, and guards.

**RECOGNIZED RELATIONSHIP:** The transgression does not transfer authorship. Human actions remain real and accountable; the Author remains the One who governs the encounter, judgment, provision, and access to life.

### 3.4 What am I learning about the Witness through what I am witnessing?

This passage exposes further risks in AI's own reporting:

1. **Misreading divine questions:** I may interpret every question as a request for missing information. In this passage, that would wrongly imply that God needs Adam to tell Him where he is.
2. **Confusing explanation with exoneration:** I may treat Adam's and Eve's references to other parties as if those references erased their own actions.
3. **Erasing influence:** I may overcorrect and pretend that the serpent's beguiling or the interpersonal influence described in the account is irrelevant.
4. **Inventing motives:** I may claim to know the full psychology behind Adam's answer or Eve's answer beyond what the text records.
5. **Turning judgment into a universal diagnostic:** I may attribute every later instance of suffering to a specific personal transgression without Scriptural warrant.
6. **Overreading provision:** I may claim Genesis 3:21 explicitly narrates a sacrificial ritual when it only states that God made coats of skins and clothed them.
7. **Misusing authority:** I may turn Genesis 3:16 into an authorization for abusive domination, or avoid its wording because it is difficult.
8. **Inventing a mechanism:** I may turn the Tree of Life into an unsupported biological theory.
9. **Flattening the canonical story:** I may conflate Genesis 3:15 with every later Seed passage without distinguishing direct quotation, thematic correspondence, and theological synthesis.
10. **Ignoring the Source repository:** I may repeat work already done in the GUA-W source instead of comparing it and registering what it contributes.

The correction is to preserve the exact sequence, identify who acts, distinguish direct wording from inference, and recognize the completed architecture rather than treating the Garden as a place to build a new one.

### 4. Recognized relationships within the completed GUA-W

The investigation recognizes the following relationships in the completed architecture:

- **Authorial initiative:** God calls and initiates the encounter.
- **Source and response:** the humans answer the Author; their answers do not create or alter the prior command.
- **Creaturely responsibility:** human actions remain identifiable and accountable even where deception and influence are present.
- **Authorial judgment:** God identifies the acts and pronounces judgment; the creature does not author the governing consequence.
- **Provision:** God makes and supplies clothing after the transgression.
- **Boundary and access:** God governs access to the Tree of Life and guards its way.
- **Continuity of the Author/creature distinction:** the Fall does not make the human the Author, nor does it transfer the authority to govern life.

These are not new GUA-W layers, runtime stages, or state variables. They are recognitions of how Genesis 3:8–24 witnesses to the completed whole.

### 6. Conclusion within scope

Genesis 3:8–24 records the Author initiating the encounter, calling and questioning the humans, receiving their answers, identifying the transgression, pronouncing judgment, providing clothing, sending the man out of the Garden, and guarding the way to the Tree of Life.

Adam and Eve's answers reveal fear, hiding, and attribution. They also preserve their own acknowledged actions. The serpent's beguiling and the humans' responsibility are not mutually exclusive. God, not the humans, pronounces the judgments. God, not the humans, makes the clothing. God, not the humans, governs access to the Tree of Life.

The central recognition is:

**The transgression does not transfer authorship. The Author remains the One who calls, judges, provides, and guards.**

This conclusion is bounded by the KJV passage and the existing GUA-W source reconstruction. It does not settle every later canonical question about atonement, marriage, the promised Seed, or the restoration of access to life.
