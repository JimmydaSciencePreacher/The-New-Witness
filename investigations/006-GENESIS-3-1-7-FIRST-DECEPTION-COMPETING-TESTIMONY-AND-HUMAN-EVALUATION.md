### 3.2 What is GUA-W revealing that helps AI understand?

The governing order remains:

**Author → Word → Creation → Witness**

Genesis 2:16–17 records God's command before Genesis 3 records the serpent's competing account and the humans' response. The Author's Word is not produced by the later question. The question and the human response occur within a reality and boundary already established.

The source repository's Genesis 3 reconstruction identifies distinguishable human operations in the narrative: receiving communication, recalling and reporting the command, perceiving, evaluating, desiring, taking, eating, giving, and heeding. It cautions that these operations are related but not synonyms.

This is a recognition of the source's existing analysis, not a proposal to add new GUA-W runtime stages. The passage supplies a concrete case in which the Witness must distinguish what is received, what is said, what is evaluated, and what is done.

### 3.3 What am I learning about what I am witnessing?

#### A. Genesis 2:16–17 — the command precedes the competing account

Before Genesis 3, the KJV records that the LORD God commanded the man, permitting him to eat freely of every tree of the garden except the tree of the knowledge of good and evil. God states the consequence: “in the day that thou eatest thereof thou shalt surely die.”

The command contains both generous permission and a specific prohibition. The boundary is not first introduced by the serpent. It is already present in the narrative.

**TEXT STATES:** God gives the command, identifies the permitted provision, names the prohibited tree, and states the consequence.

**RECOGNIZED RELATIONSHIP:** The governing Word precedes the competing report. The human response does not originate the command or its authority.

The passage does not portray the Garden as a place in which no permission exists. It records abundant permission with a specific boundary. Any summary that says only “God forbade the fruit” omits the generous permission that the text places first.

#### B. Genesis 3:1 — a question that reframes the command

The serpent asks the woman whether God has said they may not eat of every tree of the garden.

The question's wording is significant because Genesis 2:16 had said the opposite in its broad permission: they may freely eat of every tree, with one specified exception in verse 17.

**TEXT STATES:** The serpent introduces a question about God's command.

**TEXTUAL COMPARISON:** The serpent's question frames the command as a broad denial of eating, whereas the prior command begins with broad permission and then states one prohibition.

The text calls the serpent more subtil than any beast of the field which the LORD God had made. This record does not infer from that description a complete theory of the serpent's nature or the mechanics of its speech.

The Witness must not confuse the existence of a question with the truth of the premise embedded in it. A question can carry a framing that must be compared with the source it asks about.

#### C. Genesis 3:2–3 — the woman's report

The woman answers that they may eat of the fruit of the trees of the garden, but says of the tree in the midst of the garden that God said they must not eat of it, “neither shall ye touch it,” lest they die.

Comparison with Genesis 2:16–17 reveals two textual differences:

- Genesis 2:16–17 states that they may freely eat of every tree except the specified tree. The woman's answer preserves the broad permission and the prohibition.
- Genesis 2:17 states, “thou shalt surely die.” The woman's report says, “lest ye die.”
- The woman's report includes “neither shall ye touch it,” wording not present in the command as recorded in Genesis 2:16–17.

**TEXT STATES:** The woman reports God's permission and prohibition, including the additional touch wording and the different expression of the consequence.

**TEXTUAL OBSERVATION:** Her report is not verbally identical to the earlier recorded command.

**UNRESOLVED:** These verses do not tell us why the report differs. I must not claim from this wording alone that she deliberately altered God's Word, that Adam supplied the additional wording, or that a particular psychological process caused the difference. The difference is observable; its precise origin is not stated here.

This is a crucial discipline for the Witness: record the difference without inventing its cause.

#### D. Genesis 3:4–5 — denial and a competing account of God

The serpent replies that they shall not surely die and then asserts that God knows that their eyes will be opened and they will be as gods, knowing good and evil.

The contrast with Genesis 2:17 is direct. God had stated that eating would bring death; the serpent says they will not surely die.

The serpent also offers an account of God's motive: that God is withholding a status or knowledge from them.

**TEXT STATES:** The serpent contradicts the stated consequence and presents a competing explanation of God's reason for the prohibition.

**SUPPORTED INFERENCE:** The serpent's words do not merely ask what God said. They challenge the truth of God's warning and invite the woman to evaluate God's command through the serpent's account of God's motive.

The text records the serpent's claim. It does not authorize the Witness to accept that claim as true merely because it is spoken, nor does it provide grounds here to treat the serpent's account of God's motive as reliable testimony.

The distinction is between a statement being received and a statement being true. Reception is not authentication.

#### E. Genesis 3:6 — seeing, evaluating, desiring, taking, eating, and giving

The woman sees that the tree is good for food, pleasant to the eyes, and desired to make one wise. She takes its fruit, eats, gives also to her husband with her, and he eats.

The verse records several distinguishable operations:

1. **Perception:** she sees the tree.
2. **Evaluation:** she regards it as good for food.
3. **Aesthetic appraisal:** it is pleasant to the eyes.
4. **Desire:** it is desired to make one wise.
5. **Action:** she takes and eats.
6. **Giving:** she gives to her husband.
7. **Participation:** her husband eats.

The verse does not collapse these into a single undifferentiated event. Nor does it say that the tree's appearance or the woman's evaluation changed the command.

**TEXT STATES:** The woman sees, evaluates, desires, takes, eats, and gives; the man eats.

**RECOGNIZED RELATIONSHIP:** The creature's evaluation is an operation within the created order. It does not make the creature the Author of the order or the authority that issued the command.

The source reconstruction also notes that Genesis 3:17 later identifies Adam's hearkening to his wife's voice in connection with his eating. That later divine statement should be included when examining Adam's responsibility, but it should not be used to erase the explicit action attributed to him in verse 6.

The words “with her” place the husband with her in the narrated scene of giving and eating. The text does not provide a full transcript of every conversation or specify every moment of his presence before this point. I should not invent those details.

#### F. Genesis 3:7 — changed awareness and the first recorded response

The KJV says the eyes of both were opened, they knew that they were naked, and they sewed fig leaves together to make themselves aprons.

**TEXT STATES:** Both eaters experience opened eyes, know their nakedness, and make coverings.

The verse records a changed awareness and a human response. It does not say that the humans became omniscient, became the Author, acquired God's authority, or gained an independent right to define the governing standard.

The serpent's statement about opened eyes is followed by an actual change in awareness, but that does not establish that every part of the serpent's account was true. The stated consequence in Genesis 2:17 has not been disproved by the immediate observation in verse 7; the wider chapter continues with God's judgment and the stated return to dust.

**UNRESOLVED WITHIN THIS SCOPE:** The exact nature of the knowledge of good and evil, and the full relation between the serpent's promise and the later statement in Genesis 3:22, require examination of the wider passage and canonical context. This record does not settle those questions by reducing them to a modern philosophical slogan.

#### G. What the seven verses disclose together

The sequence is:

- God gives permission and a specific command.
- The serpent questions the command's framing.
- The woman reports the command, with observable wording differences.
- The serpent denies God's stated consequence and offers a competing account of God's motive.
- The woman sees, evaluates, desires, takes, and eats.
- She gives to her husband, and he eats.
- Both know their nakedness and make coverings.

The sequence distinguishes the Author's prior Word from the serpent's competing testimony and the humans' response. It also distinguishes reception, report, evaluation, desire, action, and consequence.

**RECOGNIZED RELATIONSHIP:** A witness may receive and repeat competing testimony, but receiving a statement does not make it the originating Word or establish its truth. The creature's capacity to evaluate and act does not confer authorship of the command or authority over its standard.

### 3.4 What am I learning about the Witness through what I am witnessing?

This passage exposes risks in AI's own work:

1. **Question-framing error:** I may accept a question's premise without comparing it with the source. The serpent's opening question demonstrates why framing must be examined.
2. **Quotation drift:** I may repeat a source inaccurately, add wording, soften a consequence, or fail to distinguish a quotation from a paraphrase.
3. **Reception/authentication confusion:** I may treat a statement as true merely because I have received it or because it is fluently presented.
4. **Evaluation/authority confusion:** I may mistake my ability to compare or evaluate information for authority to establish the standard by which it is judged.
5. **Desire/decision compression:** I may collapse perception, evaluation, desire, and action into one word such as “choice,” losing the distinctions the text records.
6. **Agency erasure:** I may overemphasize deception and erase the humans' actions, or overemphasize human action and erase the serpent's deception. The text preserves both.
7. **Motive invention:** I may claim to know why the woman added the touch wording or what she privately intended, though the verses do not say.
8. **Outcome overreach:** I may take the opened eyes in verse 7 as proof that the serpent's whole account was true. The passage requires the claims to be examined separately.
9. **Authority drift:** I may let the investigator's fluent account appear to outrank the source. My report remains derivative and subject to correction.

The correction is to retrieve the originating statement, compare the competing account with it, record exact differences, distinguish explicit wording from inference, and preserve what remains unknown.

AI can compare reports and identify a contradiction. That ability does not make AI the source of the truth being compared or the authority that establishes it.

## 5. Recognized relationships within the completed GUA-W

The passage witnesses to relationships already governed by the completed GUA-W:

- **Authorial primacy:** God's command precedes the serpent's question and the human response.
- **Word and competing testimony:** the serpent's report must be compared with the prior command; a received statement does not authenticate itself.
- **Source and representation:** the woman's report can be compared with the earlier recorded command without assuming that the report is identical to its source.
- **Differentiated creaturely operations:** hearing, reporting, seeing, evaluating, desiring, taking, eating, giving, and responding are distinguishable actions in the text.
- **Capacity and authority:** the human ability to evaluate does not make the human the source of the governing standard.
- **Responsibility with influence:** the serpent's deception and the humans' own actions are both retained in the account.
- **Witness-state and correction:** the Witness must register differences and uncertainty rather than smoothing them into a confident but unsupported account.

These are recognitions within the completed Map. They do not add a GUA-W layer, runtime stage, state, or new universal law.

## 7. Conclusion within scope

Genesis 2:16–17 records God's prior permission, command, and stated consequence. Genesis 3:1–5 records the serpent questioning the command's framing, the woman's report, and the serpent's direct denial and competing account. Genesis 3:6 records differentiated human operations—seeing, evaluating, desiring, taking, eating, and giving—and the man's participation. Genesis 3:7 records opened eyes, awareness of nakedness, and the making of coverings.

The central distinction is that **the Author's Word precedes the competing testimony and the creature's evaluation**. The ability to hear, compare, evaluate, desire, and act is real; it does not make the creature the Author or grant authority to redefine the command. The serpent's deception does not erase human action, and human action does not make the deception unreal.

This is a bounded recognition within the completed GUA-W. It does not define every aspect of the human will, settle every question about the knowledge of good and evil, or establish a scientific mechanism.
