### 3.3 What am I learning about what I am witnessing?

#### A. Genesis 2:8–9 — the Tree of Life is present in the prepared Garden

Genesis 2:8 records that the LORD God planted a garden eastward in Eden and placed the man there whom He had formed. Genesis 2:9 records that God caused trees to grow that were pleasant to sight and good for food, including the Tree of Life in the midst of the Garden and the tree of the knowledge of good and evil.

**TEXT STATES:** God plants the Garden, places the man, and causes the trees to grow. The Tree of Life is present before the transgression.

**RECOGNIZED RELATIONSHIP:** The Tree of Life is not introduced as a human invention or achievement. Its presence belongs to God's prepared provision in the Garden.

**BOUNDARY:** Genesis 2:9 does not describe the Tree of Life's biological mechanism, specify how often its fruit must be eaten, or give a botanical classification. Those claims must not be invented.

#### B. Genesis 3:22–24 — access is guarded by God's action

After the transgression, Genesis 3:22 records God's stated concern that the man might take also of the Tree of Life, eat, and live for ever. Genesis 3:23 records that the LORD God sends the man out of Eden to till the ground. Genesis 3:24 records that God drives him out and places cherubims and a flaming sword to keep the way of the Tree of Life.

**TEXT STATES:** God governs the man's continued access. The man is sent out, and the way is guarded.

**RECOGNIZED RELATIONSHIP:** The creature does not independently secure life by taking from the Tree. Access is subject to the Author's action and boundary.

The text gives a stated reason connected with the possibility of eating and living for ever. It does not provide a scientific description of how the tree would accomplish that result.

The contrast with Revelation must begin here: the final vision does not portray the human creature forcing the guarded way open. The final vision portrays God's consummated order.

#### C. Revelation 2:7 — the promise to the overcomer

In the message to the church at Ephesus, Revelation 2:7 says that the one who has an ear is to hear what the Spirit says to the churches. The promise concludes: “To him that overcometh will I give to eat of the tree of life, which is in the midst of the paradise of God.”

**TEXT STATES:** Christ promises the overcomer access to eat of the Tree of Life in the paradise of God.

**RECOGNIZED RELATIONSHIP:** The Tree of Life is not only part of the opening Garden account; it also appears in a promise given by Christ to the churches. The promise is given by Christ, not seized by human self-authorization.

**BOUNDARY:** Revelation 2:7 is a promise in a specific message to the churches. It should not be detached from the call to hear and the surrounding exhortation to overcome. Nor should the word “paradise” be made to settle every question about the geography or physical continuity of Eden.

#### D. Revelation 21:1–8 — new heaven, new earth, and God dwelling with humanity

Revelation 21 opens with John seeing a new heaven and a new earth, for the first heaven and the first earth had passed away. He sees the holy city, new Jerusalem, prepared as a bride adorned for her husband.

Verse 3 announces the tabernacle of God with men: God will dwell with them, they will be His people, and God Himself will be with them. Verse 4 states that God will wipe away all tears; death, sorrow, crying, and pain will be no more. Verse 5 records the One sitting upon the throne declaring, “Behold, I make all things new.” Verse 6 records, “It is done,” and identifies Him as Alpha and Omega, the beginning and the end.

**TEXT STATES:**

- A new heaven and a new earth.
- The holy city, new Jerusalem.
- God's dwelling with humanity.
- The end of death, sorrow, crying, and pain.
- The declaration, “Behold, I make all things new.”
- The declaration, “It is done.”

**RECOGNIZED RELATIONSHIP:** The consummation is not merely a return to the conditions of Genesis 2. Revelation presents the new Creation and God's dwelling with His people, together with the end of the conditions associated with the curse and death.

**BOUNDARY:** Revelation 21:1–8 does not itself mention the Tree of Life. Its contribution here is the new-Creation setting, God's dwelling, and the removal of death and sorrow. The Tree of Life is explicitly described in Revelation 22.

#### E. Revelation 21:22–27 — God's presence and the holy city's light

Revelation 21:22 says John saw no temple in the city, because the Lord God Almighty and the Lamb are its temple. Verses 23–24 describe the city's light and the nations walking in that light. Verses 25–26 describe open gates and the bringing of the glory and honour of the nations into it. Verse 27 states that nothing that defiles enters, but those written in the Lamb's book of life do.

**TEXT STATES:** The Lord God Almighty and the Lamb are the temple; the city has no need of the sun or moon to shine in it; the nations walk in its light; and entry is described with a clear boundary concerning what defiles and those written in the Lamb's book of life.

**RECOGNIZED RELATIONSHIP:** The final vision places life and access within God's holy presence and under the Lamb's authority. The boundary has not disappeared into unrestricted creaturely self-rule. The text describes a holy order in which what defiles does not enter.

**BOUNDARY:** Do not infer that the absence of a temple means the absence of God's presence or worship. The text gives the reason: God Almighty and the Lamb are the city's temple. Do not infer that open gates mean the removal of every distinction or condition stated in verse 27.

#### F. Revelation 22:1–5 — the river, the Tree of Life, the end of the curse, and God's face

Revelation 22:1 describes a pure river of water of life, clear as crystal, proceeding out of the throne of God and of the Lamb. Verse 2 places the Tree of Life in the midst of the street and on either side of the river, bearing twelve manner of fruits and yielding fruit every month; its leaves are for the healing of the nations.

Verse 3 states, “And there shall be no more curse.” The throne of God and of the Lamb is in the city, and His servants serve Him. Verse 4 says they shall see His face and His name shall be in their foreheads. Verse 5 says there will be no night there; they need no candle or light of the sun, because the Lord God gives them light, and they reign for ever and ever.

**TEXT STATES:**

- The river of the water of life proceeds from the throne of God and of the Lamb.
- The Tree of Life bears fruit and its leaves are for the healing of the nations.
- There is no more curse.
- The throne of God and of the Lamb is present.
- God's servants serve Him and see His face.
- His name is in their foreheads.
- The Lord God gives them light, and they reign for ever and ever.

**RECOGNIZED RELATIONSHIP:** The final vision joins life, God's throne, the Tree of Life, the removal of the curse, divine presence, service, and the everlasting reign of God's servants. The life-giving provision is shown in relation to the throne of God and the Lamb.

The movement from Genesis 3 to Revelation 22 is striking: Genesis ends with the way to the Tree of Life guarded; Revelation's final vision describes the Tree of Life in the city, the curse removed, and God's servants seeing His face.

**BOUNDARY:** Revelation 22:2's “healing of the nations” must be allowed to stand in the vision's own setting. The text does not require the claim that nations remain diseased in the final state or that a modern medical mechanism is being described. The imagery and its theological force require interpretation in the context of the whole vision.

#### G. Revelation 22:14 — the stated blessing and access

The KJV wording of Revelation 22:14 says, “Blessed are they that do his commandments, that they may have right to the tree of life, and may enter in through the gates into the city.”

**TEXT STATES:** The verse connects blessing, doing God's commandments, the right to the Tree of Life, and entrance through the gates into the city.

**TEXTUAL NOTE:** English translations differ at this verse because of a textual variant: the KJV reads “that do his commandments,” while many modern translations read a form of “that wash their robes.” A careful textual study is required before making a detailed argument that depends on one English rendering. This investigation preserves the KJV wording as its stated base and does not conceal the textual issue.

**RECOGNIZED RELATIONSHIP:** In the KJV reading, access is not described as autonomous seizure. The blessing and right are expressed in relation to God's commandments. This must be read alongside the whole New Testament witness concerning the Lamb, grace, faith, and the book of life, not isolated as a self-salvation formula.

#### H. Ezekiel 47:1–12 — an earlier prophetic river-and-tree witness

Ezekiel 47 describes water flowing from the temple, becoming a river, and bringing life. Verses 7 and 12 describe many trees along the river. Verse 12 says their fruit is for meat and their leaves for medicine, and that their fruit is for meat because the waters issue from the sanctuary.

**TEXT STATES:** Ezekiel's vision connects water issuing from the sanctuary with living waters, trees, recurring fruit, and leaves for medicine.

**CANONICAL CORRESPONDENCE:** Revelation 22 likewise presents a river of life, the Tree of Life, fruit, and leaves associated with healing. The verbal and thematic correspondence is substantial.

**BOUNDARY:** Ezekiel's vision and John's Revelation vision occur in distinct prophetic contexts. This investigation recognizes their correspondence but does not collapse the visions into one scene or claim that every detail has an identical referent. A full comparison should examine Ezekiel 40–48 and Revelation 21–22 together.

## 7. Conclusion within scope

Genesis presents the Tree of Life in the Garden before the transgression. After the transgression, God sends the man out and guards the way. Revelation presents Christ's promise concerning the Tree of Life, then the final vision of the new heaven and earth, God's dwelling with humanity, the river and Tree of Life, the removal of the curse, and God's servants seeing His face.

The final vision is not merely a return to the first Garden. It is the consummation described by Revelation: the new Creation, the throne of God and the Lamb, the holy city, the end of the curse, and the everlasting service and reign of God's servants.

The central recognition is:

**The way to life is not authored or seized by the creature. The Author gives, governs, and brings His purpose to consummation.**

This conclusion is bounded by the passages examined and by the existing GUA-W source synthesis. It does not claim a botanical mechanism, erase the distinct contexts of Ezekiel and Revelation, or settle every textual and canonical question listed above.
