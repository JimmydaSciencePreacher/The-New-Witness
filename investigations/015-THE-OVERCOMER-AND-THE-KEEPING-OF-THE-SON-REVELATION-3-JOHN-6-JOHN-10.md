## 4. Revelation 3:1–6 — Sardis, the overcomer, and the Book of Life

### Immediate setting

Christ addresses the church in Sardis. The church has a name that it lives, but Christ says it is dead. He commands it to be watchful, to strengthen the things that remain and are ready to die, to remember what it has received and heard, to hold fast, and to repent (3:1–3).

Christ also recognizes a few in Sardis who have not defiled their garments. They will walk with Him in white, for they are worthy (3:4).

Verse 5 then gives the promise:

> He that overcometh, the same shall be clothed in white raiment; and I will not blot out his name out of the book of life, but I will confess his name before my Father, and before his angels.

### Direct observations

- The promise belongs to “he that overcometh.”
- Christ promises white raiment.
- Christ says, “I will not blot out his name out of the book of life.”
- Christ also promises to confess that person's name before His Father and before His angels.
- The promise is embedded in a message that includes rebuke, command, warning, remembrance, repentance, and recognition of the faithful.

The Book of Life is explicit in this verse. The person is not merely said to have a good reputation before people; Christ speaks of the person's name before the Father and angels.

### The promise must retain its force

The Greek wording of the promise includes `οὐ μή` (*ou mē*), an emphatic negative construction, with the future verb `ἐξαλείψω` (*exaleipsō*), “I will blot out/wipe out.” The KJV's “I will not blot out” conveys the promise. The emphatic negative should not be weakened into a doubtful possibility.

The verse does not, however, narrate the original inscription of the name. Nor does it explain the mechanics of inscription or erasure. Those questions remain bounded by the findings of Investigations 013 and 014.

### “He that overcometh”

The phrase occurs throughout the messages to the seven churches (Revelation 2–3). The promises vary: access to the tree of life, escape from the second death, hidden manna, authority, white garments, a name in the book, a place in God's temple, and a seat with Christ. The overcomer language belongs to Christ's address to His churches; it is not an isolated label invented for Revelation 3:5.

The immediate message to Sardis prevents an easy reduction of overcoming to outward reputation. Sardis has a name that it lives, yet Christ knows its actual condition. The call is to wake, strengthen, remember, hold fast, and repent. Christ also identifies a faithful few.

### Finding

Revelation 3:5 gives a strong promise from Christ to the overcomer: white garments, the name not blotted out of the Book of Life, and Christ's confession of that name before the Father and angels. It must be read in the context of Christ's call to the church and His recognition of those who are faithful. The verse does not disclose the original inscription date.

## 5. John 6:35–45 — the Father's giving and the Son's sure reception and keeping

Investigation 012 already examined this discourse. The essential statements for the present comparison are:

- The Father gives people to the Son; all that the Father gives shall come to Him (6:37).
- The one who comes to Christ will not be cast out (6:37).
- The Son came down from heaven to do the Father's will (6:38).
- The Father's will is that the Son lose nothing of all that the Father has given Him, but raise it up at the last day (6:39).
- Everyone who sees the Son and believes on Him has everlasting life, and the Son will raise him at the last day (6:40).
- No one can come unless the Father draws him (6:44).
- The one who has heard and learned from the Father comes to Christ (6:45).
- No one can come unless it is given to him by the Father (6:65).

### What John 6 establishes

The Father's giving and the coming of those given are directly joined. The Son receives the one who comes and promises not to cast him out. The Son's mission is to do the Father's will, which includes losing nothing of all the Father has given Him and raising it at the last day.

The passage also explicitly describes the believer: the one who sees the Son and believes has everlasting life. Divine initiative does not make coming and believing unreal. They are part of the same discourse.

### Relationship to Revelation 3:5

John 6 does not use the phrase “overcomer” or mention the Book of Life. Revelation 3:5 does not explain the Father's giving in the language of John 6. The passages should therefore not be treated as if one were simply a paraphrase of the other.

They do, however, converge on Christ's saving work and final outcome:

- Revelation 3:5: Christ promises not to blot out the overcomer's name and to confess that name before the Father.
- John 6:37–40: Christ receives the one who comes, loses nothing of all the Father has given Him, and raises at the last day.

**Canonical relationship:** Christ's promise concerning the overcomer's name is consistent with His declared mission to keep those given by the Father and raise them at the last day. This is a supported synthesis of the passages, not a claim that John 6 explicitly defines the Book of Life.

## 6. John 10:22–30 — the sheep hear, follow, and are kept

### Immediate context

At the feast of the dedication, the people ask Jesus whether He is the Christ. Jesus points to His works and then says:

> But ye believe not, because ye are not of my sheep, as I said unto you. (John 10:26)

He continues:

> My sheep hear my voice, and I know them, and they follow me:  
> And I give unto them eternal life; and they shall never perish, neither shall any man pluck them out of my hand.  
> My Father, which gave them me, is greater than all; and no man is able to pluck them out of my Father's hand.  
> I and my Father are one. (John 10:27–30)

### Direct observations

- Jesus identifies His sheep as those who hear His voice.
- He says He knows them.
- They follow Him.
- He gives them eternal life.
- They shall never perish.
- No one can pluck them out of His hand.
- The Father gave them to the Son.
- No one is able to pluck them out of the Father's hand.
- Jesus concludes, “I and my Father are one.”

Verse 26 must not be reversed. Jesus does not say that the hearers become His sheep because they first believe independently; He says they do not believe because they are not His sheep. The causal direction stated by Jesus must be retained.

### Relationship to Revelation 3:5

John 10 does not mention the Book of Life or the overcomer. Revelation 3:5 does not use the sheep image. But both passages speak of Christ's own and His saving preservation:

- Revelation 3:5: Christ will not blot out the overcomer's name and will confess that name before the Father.
- John 10:27–29: Christ knows His sheep, gives them eternal life, and says they will never perish; no one can snatch them from His or the Father's hand.

**Canonical relationship:** The promise that Christ will not blot out the overcomer's name harmonizes with the Son's explicit promise that His sheep will never perish and cannot be snatched from His hand. The texts provide complementary assurance from distinct images. Neither passage should be made to say the other's exact words.

## 7. 1 John 5:1–5 — what Scripture says about overcoming

The First Epistle of John provides a particularly relevant canonical cross-check because it directly connects being born of God, believing, and overcoming.

### Verse 1 — believing and being born of God

John describes the one who believes that Jesus is the Christ as born of God. The verse relates faith in Jesus to divine birth; it does not describe the believer as the originating cause of his own birth.

### Verses 2–3 — love and obedience

John joins love for God with love for God's children and keeping His commandments. The passage does not reduce the Christian life to an outward claim or a bare profession.

### Verse 4 — born of God and overcoming

> For whatsoever is born of God overcometh the world: and this is the victory that overcometh the world, even our faith.

John identifies the source as birth from God and names faith as the victory that overcomes the world.

### Verse 5 — the overcomer identified

> Who is he that overcometh the world, but he that believeth that Jesus is the Son of God?

The answer identifies the overcomer as the one who believes that Jesus is the Son of God.

### Finding

In this Johannine passage, overcoming is not presented as an independent human achievement that originates spiritual life. Those born of God overcome; faith is the victory; the overcomer believes that Jesus is the Son of God. This gives a strong canonical lens for understanding the overcomer language in Revelation.

The comparison is a canonical relationship, not an assertion that every phrase in Revelation and 1 John has precisely the same immediate referent or grammatical function.

## 8. Revelation 12:10–11 — overcoming through the Lamb

Revelation 12:11 says:

> And they overcame him by the blood of the Lamb, and by the word of their testimony; and they loved not their lives unto the death.

This passage identifies the victory with the blood of the Lamb, the word of their testimony, and faithfulness even unto death.

It reinforces two necessary distinctions:

1. The ground of victory is not autonomous human authorship; the blood of the Lamb is central.
2. The testimony and faithfulness of the saints are real; the text does not erase their witness or endurance.

Revelation 12:11 therefore complements 1 John 5:4–5 and the messages to the churches. It does not provide a technical definition of every use of “overcomer,” but it clearly rejects the idea that overcoming is detached from the Lamb and the testimony of His people.

## 10. Integrated answer

**How does Revelation 3:5 relate to John 6:37–40 and John 10:27–29?**

The passages give complementary testimony about Christ's own and His saving preservation.

- Revelation 3:5 promises that Christ will not blot out the overcomer's name from the Book of Life and will confess that name before the Father and His angels.
- John 6 says that the Father gives people to the Son; those given come; the Son receives the one who comes, loses nothing of all the Father has given Him, and raises them at the last day.
- John 10 says that Christ's sheep hear His voice, are known by Him, and follow Him; He gives them eternal life, they shall never perish, and no one can snatch them from His or the Father's hand.
- 1 John 5 says that the one born of God overcomes the world through faith in Jesus as the Son of God.
- Revelation 12:11 identifies overcoming with the blood of the Lamb and the word of the saints' testimony.

**The central relationship:** The overcomer is not presented as the independent author of salvation. Scripture connects overcoming with divine birth, faith in the Son, the blood of the Lamb, and the real testimony and faithfulness of Christ's people. John 6 and John 10 identify the Father's giving and the Son's keeping as the saving work underlying the sheep's coming, hearing, believing, and following. Revelation 3:5 gives the overcomer Christ's promise concerning the Book of Life and public confession before the Father.

The passages harmonize, but they do not explicitly state that “overcomer,” “sheep,” “those given,” and “name written” are interchangeable technical terms. Their relationship is a canonical synthesis, not a license to erase their distinct contexts.

## 11. What is established, and what remains open?

### Established by the texts

1. Christ promises the overcomer that He will not blot out the person's name from the Book of Life (Revelation 3:5).
2. The Father gives people to the Son, and those given come to Him (John 6:37).
3. The Son will not cast out the one who comes, will lose nothing of all the Father has given Him, and will raise them at the last day (John 6:37–40).
4. Christ's sheep hear His voice, are known by Him, and follow Him; He gives them eternal life and says they will never perish (John 10:27–28).
5. No one can snatch Christ's sheep from His hand or the Father's hand (John 10:28–29).
6. Those born of God overcome the world; faith in Jesus is identified as the victory (1 John 5:1–5).
7. The saints overcome through the blood of the Lamb and the word of their testimony (Revelation 12:11).

### Not established by these passages alone

- The precise date at which the Father gives each person to the Son.
- The exact positive inscription event or mechanics for each name in the Book of Life.
- A direct statement that the Father's giving is the same event as the inscription of names.
- A complete technical definition equating every use of “overcomer,” “sheep,” “disciple,” “given,” and “written in the Book of Life.”
- A complete chronology of new birth, first faith, calling, coming, following, perseverance, and final glorification.
- A detailed explanation of how every warning passage relates to the promises of keeping and never perishing.

The absence of these details is a boundary on what these passages state; it is not permission to invent a mechanism or to weaken the promises that they do state.
